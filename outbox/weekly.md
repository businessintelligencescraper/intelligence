# Corporate Affairs Intelligence — Weekly Briefing — Week of September 28–October 4, 2026

## The week in one paragraph

The most serious development was a multistate E. coli outbreak tied to Sierra Nevada Graziers raw-milk cheese, with 13 illnesses across nine states and three cases of hemolytic uremic syndrome concentrated in young children — a reminder of the category-wide scrutiny cheese-safety incidents draw even when the implicated product sits outside conventional pasteurized dairy. ## Top developments

**Multistate E. coli Outbreak Tied to Sierra Nevada Graziers Raw Milk Cheese Prompts Recall** (High · Executive Awareness Required)
FDA and CDC are investigating a multistate E. coli O26:H11 outbreak linked to Sierra Nevada Graziers grass-fed raw milk cheese; as of Sept. 25, 13 people across nine states have been sickened, 8 hospitalized, and 3 have developed hemolytic uremic syndrome, with more than half of those sickened age 5 or younger. *Why it matters:* A severe outbreak with HUS cases in young children and national broadcast coverage keeps cheese-safety scrutiny in the news, even though the implicated product is unpasteurized raw-milk cheese outside a conventional pasteurized-dairy product line. *Recommended action:* Brief food-safety and communications leadership; confirm there is no supply-chain or private-label link; monitor for further FDA/CDC updates. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-e-coli-o26h11-raw-milk-cheese-september-2026), [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/ecoli/outbreaks/raw-milk-cheese-09-26/investigation.html), [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/GMA/News/coli-outbreak-linked-raw-milk-cheese-brand-cdc/story?id=136766868)

**GOP Senators Break With White House as ICE Crackdown Tied to Fatal Kansas Dairy-Facility Incident Spreads Across Cattle Belt** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required)
A multi-day ICE enforcement operation that began around Sept. 23 across southwest Kansas dairy and meatpacking facilities widened this week into Texas and Oklahoma; national coverage tied its origin to an Aug. 31 fatal stabbing at a Kansas heifer-rearing facility, cattle-industry groups report slaughter volumes down 14–16% week over week, and Kansas Republican Sens. Marshall and Moran have publicly urged ICE to coordinate with local law enforcement. *Why it matters:* The crackdown's spread, its origin at a facility feeding future milking herds, and public discomfort from Kansas's own GOP delegation raise workforce-continuity and political risk for a milk-supply chain NMPF estimates is roughly 79% dependent on immigrant labor. *Recommended action:* Update supply-chain and government-affairs leadership on the widening scope; continue monitoring for spread to other sourcing regions or a formal industry statement. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/24/politics/republicans-suddenly-concerned-about-ice), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/26/ice-raids-trump-meatpackers-revenue-losses-texas-kansas-oklahoma-republicans-marshall-moran/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/ice-activity-disrupting-thousands-of-cattle-ranchers-say)

**Canadian Whey Protein Makers Describe "Devastating" Impact Ahead of Sept. 29 US Import Ban** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required)
Canadian whey protein manufacturers are stockpiling inventory and evaluating substitutes as the Section 338 US import ban on Canadian dairy products, including whey, takes full effect Sept. 29; one company borrowed money to stockpile six months of whey, and industry voices say substitutes like pea protein aren't easy swaps. *Why it matters:* Confirms real supply-chain strain among Canadian dairy-ingredient suppliers just ahead of the ban, reinforcing sourcing-risk exposure for any US processor relying on Canadian-origin whey, milk protein concentrate, lactose or casein. *Recommended action:* Brief supply-chain/procurement leadership on ingredient-sourcing exposure; continue monitoring for a negotiated resolution or legal challenge. [CNN Business](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/27/business/whey-protein-trade-war)

**Conservative Social-Media Boycott Targets Tillamook Over Halal-Certified Cheese Labeling** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required)
Conservative social-media accounts launched a boycott campaign against Tillamook after noticing a "Certified Halal" mark on its cheese packaging; the certification reflects a 2016 switch to fermentation-produced rennet, not a recipe change, and fact-checkers including Snopes have since called the boycott's premise misleading, with no confirmed sales impact. *Why it matters:* Fermentation-produced rennet carrying halal certification is common industry-wide, so a similarly labeled product from any national dairy manufacturer could draw comparable backlash even where the underlying practice is routine. *Recommended action:* Confirm messaging is ready in case similar rennet/certification practices draw the same scrutiny. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/9/18/critics-in-meltdown-over-us-cheese-giant-tillamook-halal-label), [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/tillamook-cheese-halal-boycott/)

**California's 'Non-Ultraprocessed Certified' Label Bill Was Not Actually Signed — EWG Correction** (Low · Executive Awareness Required)
The Environmental Working Group, AB 2244's co-sponsor, confirmed its earlier release announcing Gov. Newsom had signed the bill was premature; no signature is on record, and Newsom has until Sept. 30, 2026 to act. *Why it matters:* The decision on the first state certification seal built around a legal ultra-processed-food definition — with criteria touching flavored dairy and processed cheese — remains genuinely open with days left on the clock. *Recommended action:* Correct any internal briefings that cited the bill as signed; continue monitoring for the actual signature or veto by Sept. 30. [The Fence Post](https://www.thefencepost.com/news/newsom-has-not-signed-non-ultraprocessed-foods-label/)

**Large Umbrella Review Links Ultra-Processed Food Intake to Broad Range of Health Risks** (Medium · Potential Media Interest)
A new umbrella review of 51 studies covering more than 8.8 million participants, published in the BMJ journal Family Medicine and Community Health, found higher ultra-processed food intake associated with increased cardiovascular disease, cancer, obesity, diabetes and all-cause mortality risk. *Why it matters:* One of the largest UPF evidence syntheses to date is likely to draw broad general-media coverage and adds momentum to the wider UPF-labeling policy debate, though it does not name dairy specifically. *Recommended action:* Monitor media pickup and have standard dairy-nutrition/UPF-classification talking points on hand. [FoodNavigator USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/09/23/upf-health-risks-examined-in-review-of-more-than-8-million-people/)

**USDA Sends Mandatory Dairy Manufacturing Cost Survey Proposed Rule to OMB for Review** (Medium · Monitor Only)
USDA's Agricultural Marketing Service submitted a proposed rule implementing the mandatory dairy-processing cost and yield survey authorized by the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act to OMB/OIRA for review, classified "economically significant." *Why it matters:* Would require dairy processing plants to disclose detailed manufacturing cost and yield data feeding into Federal Milk Marketing Order make-allowance calculations; OIRA review is typically the last step before Federal Register publication and a comment period. *Recommended action:* Monitor for Federal Register publication; no action needed until reporting mechanics are released. [OMB/OIRA](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eoReviewSearch)

**Schreiber Foods to Invest $267 Million in New Missouri Process-Cheese Plant** (Low · Monitor Only)
Schreiber Foods announced a $267 million investment to build a new process-cheese facility in Carthage, Missouri, expected to open in 2028 and add about 100 jobs. *Why it matters:* A major competitor's large capacity expansion signals continued confidence in process-cheese demand and adds regional competition for Midwest milk supply and labor. [Cheese Reporter](https://cheesereporter.com/news/production-supply-chain/2026/09/25/schreiber-foods-to-invest-over-267-million-to-build-new-process-cheese-facility-in-missouri/)

DMI, which administers the national dairy checkoff, named Corey Scott — currently CEO of Midwest Dairy — as its next president and CEO, succeeding Barbara O'Brien following DMI's Oct. 19–21 Joint Annual Meeting. *Why it matters:* A leadership transition atop the organization that runs checkoff-funded stewardship and promotion programs, coming weeks after USDA restricted checkoff funding of ESG programming, is worth tracking for how the incoming CEO positions those programs. [Agri-Pulse](https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/25286-dairy-management-inc-names-corey-scott-president-and-ceo)

**Wholesaler-Distributor Trade Group Appeals Oregon Packaging-EPR Ruling to the Ninth Circuit** (Low · Monitor Only)

[NAW](https://www.naw.org/naw-appeals-oregon-epr-ruling-ninth-circuit/)

**CoBank: Higher-Component Dairy Herds Earn Up to $474 More Per Cow** (Low · Monitor Only)
A CoBank analysis finds dairy herds in the top decile for milk components (butterfat and protein) earn up to $474 more per cow annually, as more than 80% of farmgate milk now moves into manufactured dairy products rather than fluid milk. *Why it matters:* Relevant background for sourcing and producer-relations strategy as the category mix keeps shifting toward manufactured products.
