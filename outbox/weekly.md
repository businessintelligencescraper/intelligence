# Corporate Affairs Intelligence — Weekly Briefing — Week of September 7–13, 2026

## The week in one paragraph

The most consequential thread this week ran through infant-formula safety: CDC and FDA formally closed a second multistate infant botulism outbreak and traced it to the same two suppliers implicated in the 2025 ByHeart outbreak, and then a foundation report meant to review both outbreaks turned out to have been funded by the implicated company and the dairy industry's own trade association — a credibility problem for industry-funded safety science that extends beyond the companies directly named. Separately, California became the first state to pass a certification framework for "Non-Ultraprocessed" food labeling, sending AB 2244 to the Governor with a decision due by September 30, while a new consumer survey quantified what recall-heavy years have been costing food brands: two-thirds of shoppers now say they've avoided an entire food category after just one recall notice. On the investment side, Chobani announced a $1.2 billion plant conversion in Pennsylvania — one of the largest dairy-processing capacity commitments of the year — and a bipartisan pair of House bills advanced that would create a single federal standard for packaging "recyclable" and "recycled content" claims, offering relief from a growing patchwork of state rules even as the approach draws early greenwashing criticism. Routine trade-policy and personnel news rounded out the week: updated 2027 dairy import-license fees and quotas, a reopened EPA comment window on wetlands jurisdiction, and the retirement announcement of NMPF's longtime trade-policy chief.

## Top developments

**Report Reviewing Industry's Infant-Formula Botulism Outbreaks Was Funded by the Implicated Company and Trade Group** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — The Reagan-Udall Foundation for the FDA published a 44-page report on preventing Clostridium botulinum contamination in powdered infant formula, stemming from a July roundtable that was convened in collaboration with, and paid for by, ByHeart and the International Dairy Foods Association (IDFA) — the company and trade association tied to outbreaks that hospitalized roughly 50 infants. None of the report's 24 contributors was a parent of an affected infant.
*Why it matters:* IDFA speaks for the dairy manufacturing industry broadly, so criticism that the trade group and an implicated company funded a review of their own outbreak is a reputational exposure that can extend to dairy processors generally, especially around industry-funded science and testing-standard credibility.
*Recommended action:* Brief communications/government-affairs leadership on the funding-transparency criticism before reporters ask whether the company backs IDFA's role in it. [Reagan-Udall Foundation](https://reaganudall.org/publications/applying-science-infant-formula-safety), [Marler Blog](https://www.marlerblog.com/case-news/byheart-and-the-dairy-industry-paid-for-a-report-on-the-formula-botulism-outbreaks-it-has-22-recommendations-no-parent-in-the-room-and-no-rule/)

**California Legislature Passes First-of-Its-Kind 'Non-Ultraprocessed Certified' Food Label Bill** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — Assembly Bill 2244 cleared the Legislature unanimously (32-0 Senate, 72-0 Assembly) and was sent to Governor Newsom, who has until Sept. 30 to sign or veto it and is reportedly expected to sign. The bill creates a voluntary state certification seal for products meeting standards that exclude synthetic dyes, flavor enhancers, non-sugar sweeteners and certain processing aids.
*Why it matters:* This would be the first state-level certification built around a UPF definition and a template other states may copy; the qualifying criteria touch additives common in flavored milk, cultured dairy and processed cheese.
*Recommended action:* Brief labeling/regulatory affairs on AB 2244 ahead of the Sept. 30 deadline; assess which products could qualify for the seal and which would be excluded. [BakeryAndSnacks](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/09/01/california-passes-non-upf-food-certification-bill/), [The Shelby Report](https://theshelbyreport.com/2026/09/01/calif-bill-creating-non-ultraprocessed-food-label-sent-to-governor/)

**CDC/FDA Close Second Infant-Formula Botulism Outbreak, Trace It to Same Dairy Supplier as Prior Outbreak** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — CDC and FDA formally closed the investigation into an outbreak linked to Nara Organics powdered infant formula (four infants hospitalized, no deaths), tracing contamination via whole-genome sequencing to the same two suppliers implicated in the 2025 ByHeart outbreak: Organic West Milk and Dairy Farmers of America. FDA also found Organic West Milk had earlier given the agency an incomplete customer list that omitted Nara Organics.
*Why it matters:* This is the second national infant-health outbreak traced to the same organic whole-milk supply chain within about a year, and a supplier reportedly withholding a customer name during a federal investigation raises the likelihood of tighter agency scrutiny of organic dairy ingredient sourcing and supplier traceability industry-wide.
*Recommended action:* Brief supply-chain/quality leadership on the supplier link; confirm no shared sourcing exposure. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/botulism/outbreaks-investigations/infant-formula-june-2026/index.html), [Food Safety News](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2026/08/federal-officials-close-investigation-into-outbreak-of-infant-botulism-traced-to-formula/)

**Survey Finds Rising Recall Volume Is Eroding Consumer Trust and Buying Behavior** (Medium · Potential Media Interest) — A GS1 US-commissioned survey found FDA posted 26 food recall notices in August 2026 alone, and that 67% of consumers now say they've avoided an entire food category after just one recall notice, up from 60% a year earlier; 94% say they're concerned about recall frequency, and trust that the recall system protects public health fell to 80% from 85%.
*Why it matters:* Dairy products have accounted for a meaningful share of 2026's recall notices, so this data quantifies a real category-avoidance risk tied to recall volume and reinforces the case for clear, fast recall communications.
*Recommended action:* Share findings with communications and consumer-insights teams; use as supporting data when reviewing recall-response messaging. [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/02/food-recalls-fda-americans-avoid-food-categories/), [Food Safety Magazine](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/11782-survey-says-94-percent-of-us-adults-concerned-about-frequency-of-food-recalls)

**Chobani to Invest $1.2 Billion in New Pennsylvania Milk-Processing Hub** (Medium · Potential Media Interest) — Chobani will invest roughly $1.2 billion to convert a former Keurig Dr Pepper plant in Allentown, Pennsylvania into a 1.5-million-square-foot milk production and warehouse campus, adding more than 900 jobs and, at full capacity, sourcing over 3 billion pounds of Pennsylvania milk annually — about 30% of the state's current production. As part of the deal, Chobani is buying back Keurig Dr Pepper's roughly $800 million equity stake in the company.
*Why it matters:* A major new capacity buildout by a leading yogurt/dairy processor signals rising competition for Northeast milk supply and expanding category scale; no direct regulatory exposure, but worth tracking for regional sourcing and pricing dynamics.
*Recommended action:* Track regional milk-sourcing and capacity dynamics in the Northeast; no direct action needed. [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/Chobani-plant-investment-keurig-dr-pepper-growth/829283/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/chobani-plans-milk-expansion-with-1-2-billion-plant-investment)

**House Subcommittee Advances Bipartisan Bills to Federalize Packaging 'Recyclable' and 'Recycled Content' Claims** (Medium · Monitor Only) — The House Energy and Commerce Committee's Commerce, Manufacturing and Trade Subcommittee voice-voted to advance the PACK Act and the Recycled Materials Attribution Act to the full committee. The bills would set federal, third-party-certified standards for "recyclable," "compostable," "reusable" and "recycled content" packaging claims, preempting the current patchwork of state rules; the RMAA would also formally recognize mass-balance accounting for recycled-content claims.
*Why it matters:* A single federal packaging-claims standard would ease compliance for a national manufacturer navigating inconsistent state EPR and labeling laws, but the mass-balance provision is already drawing greenwashing criticism from environmental advocates.
*Recommended action:* Monitor for full-committee markup or greenwashing criticism naming specific brands or claims. [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/policy-now/2026/09/01/house-subcommittee-advances-pack-act/), [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/09/04/cpgs-eye-relief-from-packaging-claims-patchwork/)

**USDA Sets 2027 Dairy Import License Fee and Quota Terms** (Low · Monitor Only) — USDA's Foreign Agricultural Service published Federal Register notices setting the 2027 dairy tariff-rate-quota import license fee at $325 (down from $350), updating safeguard trigger levels and license-transfer amounts, and shifting some quota amounts from the historical to the lottery license category for 2027.
*Why it matters:* Routine annual administrative filings, not a policy change, but they set the mechanics for who can import quota-limited dairy ingredients in 2027, worth noting given the active US-Canada dairy tariff dispute.
*Recommended action:* No action needed; forward to trade/import compliance as a 2027 planning reference. [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/02/2026-17954/assessment-of-fees-for-dairy-import-licenses-for-the-2027-tariff-rate-import-quota-year), [Cheese Reporter](https://cheesereporter.com/news/policy-legislation/2026/09/03/usda-reduces-2027-dairy-import-license-fee-announces-safeguard-trigger-levels-license-transfer-amounts/)

**EPA and Army Corps Reopen Public Comment on WOTUS Water Definition Ahead of Final Rule** (Low · Monitor Only) — EPA and the Army Corps issued a Supplemental Notice of Proposed Rulemaking seeking additional input on how to define "waters of the United States" under the Clean Water Act, including "relatively permanent" waters and "continuous surface water connection." The agencies say they're advancing toward a final rule after more than 22,000 comments on their original November 2025 proposal.
*Why it matters:* WOTUS jurisdiction determines which farm ditches, wetlands and on-site waters require Clean Water Act permits, directly affecting dairy farms and processing facilities that manage wastewater or stormwater.
*Recommended action:* Monitor for the Federal Register publication and confirmed comment deadline. [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-and-army-seek-additional-input-proposed-waters-us-definition-while-advancing), [Agri-Pulse](https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/25198-epa-corps-issue-supplemental-wotus-proposal)

**IDFA-Backed SNAP Milk Incentive Program "Add Milk!" Expands to Oklahoma** (Low · Potential Media Interest) — The Healthy Fluid Milk Incentive project "Add Milk!", championed by IDFA and funded via a USDA grant to Auburn University, launched at three Tulsa-area stores, giving SNAP participants a dollar-for-dollar match on qualifying low-fat, non-fat and lactose-free milk purchases. The expansion brings the program to more than 1,200 stores across 32 states.
*Why it matters:* A positive, verifiable industry-backed nutrition-access story that gives dairy manufacturers a concrete talking point on milk affordability and SNAP access.
*Recommended action:* Usable as a proof point in nutrition-access/affordability messaging. [Cheese Reporter](https://cheesereporter.com/news/food-safety-health/2026/09/04/snap-milk-incentive-program-expands-to-oklahoma/)

**USDA Unveils Plan to Modernize Agricultural Data Collection** (Low · Monitor Only) — USDA Secretary Brooke Rollins announced a "Data Modernization Plan" aimed at reducing producer reporting burden, expanding use of administrative data and mobile survey access, and modernizing data infrastructure and geospatial tools.
*Why it matters:* USDA collects extensive mandatory and voluntary data from dairy processors; how the plan is implemented could change reporting formats, frequency or burden, though no specific new dairy requirement was announced.
*Recommended action:* Monitor for specifics on how the plan applies to existing USDA dairy reporting programs. [USDA](https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/news/press-releases/2026/09/01/secretary-rollins-unveils-plan-modernize-agricultural-data-collection-and-put-farmers-first)

**NMPF's Longtime Trade-Policy Chief Jaime Castaneda to Retire in December** (Low · Monitor Only) — National Milk Producers Federation announced that Executive Vice President Jaime Castaneda, who has led NMPF and the U.S. Dairy Export Council's international trade-policy work since 1999, will retire in December 2026. He plans to continue as Executive Director of the Consortium for Common Food Names.
*Why it matters:* Castaneda has been a lead architect of US dairy's export and trade-policy positioning, including USMCA dairy-access disputes, and his exit lands during an active period of US-Canada tariff negotiations. NMPF has not announced a successor.
*Recommended action:* Monitor for a successor announcement. [NMPF](https://www.nmpf.org/long-time-dairy-advocate-jaime-castaneda-announces-retirement-plans/)
