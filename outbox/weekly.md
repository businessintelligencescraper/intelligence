# Corporate Affairs Intelligence — Weekly Briefing — Week of November 23–29, 2026

## The week in one paragraph

H5N1 in dairy cattle kept expanding and moved further into human-health territory this week: Oklahoma reported its first dairy-herd case since 2024, and Oregon confirmed its first human case of the year, tied to the same Morrow County herd behind the state's first cattle detection. With at least three new states (Indiana, Oregon, Oklahoma) reporting first-time dairy-herd cases within two weeks, state animal-health regulators formally asked USDA to authorize an emergency vaccine pilot for dairy cattle — a request that, if granted, would be the first authorized H5N1 vaccine option for the US dairy herd. Separately, New York's self-affirmed-GRAS food-chemical disclosure bill reached Governor Hochul's desk, starting a 10-day clock that expires November 25; the outcome is expected to set a template other states are watching closely. On a quieter note, FDA and CDC formally closed the multistate E. coli outbreak linked to Sierra Nevada Graziers raw-milk cheese, with no residual product on the market. None of the week's developments were rated High risk, and officials continue to emphasize that pasteurized milk and dairy products remain unaffected by the bird-flu cases.

## Top developments

**New York's GRAS Food-Chemical Disclosure Bill Formally Reaches Governor Hochul's Desk, Starting Veto Clock** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — New York's Legislature formally delivered the Food Safety and Chemical Disclosure Act (S.1239/A.1556) to Gov. Hochul on Nov. 13, starting her 10-day (excluding Sundays) window to sign or veto; absent action by Nov. 25 it becomes law without her signature. The bill bans three additives (Red No. 3, potassium bromate, propylparaben) and would be the first state law requiring public disclosure of the safety basis for self-affirmed-GRAS ingredients. *Why it matters:* this is the concrete deadline the GRAS-disclosure fight has been building toward all year, with industry urging a veto and consumer advocates pressing for a signature; the outcome would set a template other states could follow for ingredients used across packaged dairy products. [PackagingLaw.com](https://www.packaginglaw.com/news/new-york-gras-bill-formally-presented-governor)

**Oregon Confirms State's First Human H5N1 Case, Tied to Dairy-Herd Outbreak** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — The Oregon Health Authority confirmed the state's first human H5N1 case of 2026 in a person linked to the Morrow County dairy herd confirmed infected Nov. 10; the patient had mild symptoms, was not hospitalized, and received oseltamivir. *Why it matters:* the fourth documented US human H5N1 case tied to dairy cattle this year, extending the outbreak's human-health footprint into a new state and keeping worker-safety and biosecurity questions live for the dairy category, even though pasteurized milk remains unaffected. [KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/news/2026/11/19/oregon-reports-first-human-case-of-bird-flu-in-2026/), [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/science/articles/human-contracts-bird-flu-morrow-200924654.html)

**Oklahoma Confirms First H5N1 Dairy-Herd Case Since 2024** (Medium · Potential Media Interest) — USDA APHIS confirmed H5N1 in an Oklahoma dairy herd of roughly 3,000–4,000 cows on Nov. 16, the state's first dairy-cattle case since 2024. *Why it matters:* at least the third state to report its first dairy-herd detection within two weeks, extending a fall resurgence that keeps biosecurity and consumer-confidence questions live, though officials emphasize pasteurization keeps the commercial milk supply safe. [High Plains Journal](https://hpj.com/2026/11/16/hpai-confirmed-in-oklahoma-dairy-herd/), [KOSU](https://www.kosu.org/local-news/2026-11-17/bird-flu-found-in-oklahoma-dairy-herd)

**State Animal-Health Officials Press USDA to Authorize Emergency H5N1 Vaccine Pilot for Dairy Cattle** (Medium · Potential Media Interest) — NASDA and the National Assembly of State Animal Health Officials sent USDA a letter asking it to authorize a voluntary national emergency-use vaccination framework for H5N1 in dairy cattle, starting with a pilot in willing states; the outbreak has reached at least 22 states with nearly 1,200 herds testing positive since March 2024, and no vaccine is yet approved for US dairy cattle. *Why it matters:* a formal request from state regulators (not just industry) raises the odds USDA moves on emergency authorization; any vaccination program would carry trade and export-access implications alongside biosecurity benefits. [Feedstuffs](https://www.feedstuffs.com/dairy/state-organizations-urge-usda-to-authorize-dairy-hpai-vaccine), [Farm Progress](https://www.farmprogress.com/tv-and-radio/state-ag-leaders-push-usda-for-dairy-vaccine-option)

**FDA and CDC Declare Sierra Nevada Graziers Raw-Milk Cheese E. coli Outbreak Over** (Low · Monitor Only) — FDA and CDC closed out the multistate E. coli O26:H11 outbreak linked to Sierra Nevada Graziers raw-milk cheese, first identified in September; the final tally is 18 illnesses across 10 states, with no new illnesses since the recall. *Why it matters:* closes the loop on a story that was rated High risk at the outset, with a clean resolution and no residual product on the market — a modest positive data point for raw-milk-cheese safety messaging, though the warning-label citizen petition this outbreak helped prompt remains open at FDA. [Food Safety News](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2026/11/fda-reports-one-outbreak-has-ended-investigations-into-others-continue/), [FDA](https://fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-e-coli-o26h11-raw-milk-cheese-september-2026)
