# Corporate Affairs Intelligence — Weekly Briefing — Week of August 31 – September 6, 2026

## The week in one paragraph

The US-Canada dairy trade dispute moved from threat to specifics this week: Canada published its full retaliatory tariff list on Aug. 25, confirming a differentiated 25% duty on cheese and finished dairy products and a steeper 50% duty on dairy ingredients (whey, caseinates, lactose, milk/cream), effective Sept. 8, while national trade associations publicly urged Washington to keep pressing Canada on separate, longer-running USMCA compliance disputes. On the regulatory side, a federal judge upheld Oregon's packaging extended-producer-responsibility law as constitutional in the first full trial test of any state EPR statute — a result that reduces near-term litigation risk for similar laws in six other states. The dairy industry notched a second consecutive voluntary school-nutrition win, completing a pledge to remove artificial colors from school milk, cheese and yogurt, even as processing consolidation continued with a Wisconsin cheese-plant closure set against a reported $13 billion in sector-wide capacity investment over the past year. Separately, a multiyear Listeria outbreak tied to soft cheese was formally declared over, a New World screwworm case cluster near the Arizona border grew but has not disrupted the newly reopened cattle-import corridor, and opposition to FDA's pending ultra-processed-food definition broadened to include free-market and consumer-rights groups alongside industry.

## Top developments

**Canada Publishes Retaliatory Tariff List: US Dairy and Cheese Exports Face 25% Duties Starting Sept. 8** (High · Executive Awareness Required) — Canada published the full product list and rates for its "dollar for dollar" retaliatory tariffs on Aug. 25, covering more than 700 US goods worth roughly $20 billion. Dairy and cheese products face a 25% duty, dairy ingredients (whey, caseinates, lactose, milk/cream) face 50%, steel and aluminum face 50%, and most remaining items are set at 15%; the tariffs take effect Sept. 8.
*Why it matters:* This is the first confirmed, quantified rate for the dairy-sector retaliation Canada flagged on Aug. 22 — a materially different exposure picture for ingredient exporters (50%) versus finished-cheese exporters (25%), effective in under two weeks with no resumed-talks timeline announced.
*Recommended action:* Brief leadership on the confirmed rates ahead of the Sept. 8 effective date; assess Canada-bound export exposure and pricing implications now that specific rates are public. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/25/canada-trump-tariffs-trade-carney-leblanc.html), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/canada-retaliates-against-trump-tariffs-rcna594280)

**NMPF and USDEC Thank Administration for Continued Pressure on Canada Over USMCA Dairy Compliance** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — Following the Aug. 22 US tariffs on Canadian-origin dairy ingredients, NMPF and USDEC publicly thanked the administration for continuing to press Canada, and flagged two longer-running USMCA compliance grievances — chronic underfill of Canada's dairy tariff-rate quotas and loopholes on dairy protein exports — running on a separate track from the Sept. 8 retaliation deadline.
*Why it matters:* Confirms the industry's national associations are backing continued federal pressure even after tariffs took effect, and signals this dispute has multiple tracks that could stay active regardless of how the Sept. 8 retaliation plays out.
*Recommended action:* No new action beyond existing Canada-tariff monitoring; track the TRQ-underfill and dairy-protein-loophole grievances as a second compliance track. [NMPF](https://www.nmpf.org/usdec-nmpf-thank-administration-for-maintaining-pressure-on-canada-to-resolve-usmca-dairy-concerns/)

**Federal Judge Upholds Oregon's Packaging EPR Law as Constitutional** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — In a 71-page ruling issued Aug. 27, a U.S. District judge rejected the National Association of Wholesaler-Distributors' challenge to Oregon's packaging EPR law, finding it does not violate the Dormant Commerce Clause or Due Process Clause — the first full federal trial to test an EPR packaging law's constitutionality on a complete record.
*Why it matters:* This was the bellwether case for all seven states with packaging EPR laws; upholding it makes similar challenges elsewhere harder to win, reinforcing that EPR compliance costs facing packaged dairy manufacturers are likely durable. An appeal is expected.
*Recommended action:* Brief packaging/sustainability leadership that litigation risk to EPR compliance costs has decreased for now; watch for a Ninth Circuit appeal. [Waste Dive](https://www.wastedive.com/news/oregon-epr-law-naw-constitution-judge-ruling/829044/)

**Sonora Screwworm Case Count Grows to Nine as Arizona Cattle-Border Reopening Proceeds** (Medium · Monitor Only) — Mexico's Sonora state registered nine confirmed New World screwworm cases in the week since its first Aug. 19 detection, with newer cases near the Chihuahua border, more than 200 miles from the Douglas, AZ crossing that reopened to cattle imports Aug. 24. USDA has not changed the phased reopening plan.
*Why it matters:* The case count grew nine-fold in a week after USDA already chose not to delay the reopening for the initial detection; continued growth keeps the newly reopened cattle supply chain feeding US dairy replacement heifers one adverse development from a renewed suspension.
*Recommended action:* Monitor for any USDA statement on the reopening's status; no action needed now. [KJZZ](https://www.kjzz.org/fronteras-desk/2026-08-27/sonora-registers-more-screwworm-cases-as-cattle-imports-to-arizona-resume)

**EWG Report Finds Chipotle Only Fast-Food Chain Free of Ultra-Processed Ingredients Among 12 Reviewed** (Medium · Potential Media Interest) — The Environmental Working Group analyzed 12 major fast-food chains for ultra-processed ingredients and found Chipotle the only one with none, while separately noting cheese as the top dietary source of saturated fat and citing antibiotic use in dairy production as a related concern.
*Why it matters:* Adds cheese-specific framing to the broader, already-tracked ultra-processed-foods narrative from a prominent advocacy group with regular media pickup, ahead of FDA's pending UPF definition.
*Recommended action:* Monitor media pickup; no direct response needed. [EWG](https://www.ewg.org/news-insights/news/2026/08/we-checked-12-fast-food-chains-ultra-processed-ingredients-only-one-had)

**Dairy Industry Completes Pledge to Remove Artificial Colors From School Milk, Cheese and Yogurt** (Low · Potential Media Interest) — Dairy processors fulfilled IDFA's voluntary "Healthy Dairy in Schools Commitment," eliminating seven certified artificial colors from milk, cheese and yogurt sold into school meal programs ahead of the 2026-2027 school year — the second consecutive year the industry has delivered on a voluntary school-nutrition pledge.
*Why it matters:* A positive, verifiable compliance story that gives dairy manufacturers a concrete talking point amid ongoing scrutiny of artificial food dyes.
*Recommended action:* Usable as a proof point in dye- or school-nutrition-related messaging. [Dairy Foods](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/99348-dairy-industry-meets-commitment-to-remove-artificial-colors-from-school-dairy-products)

**Foremost Farms to Close Wisconsin Cheese Plant, Cutting 68 Jobs** (Low · Monitor Only) — Foremost Farms USA will permanently close its Marshfield, Wisconsin cheese-processing plant by Dec. 15, 2026, eliminating all 68 jobs, per WARN Act notices filed Aug. 26.
*Why it matters:* Another data point in ongoing dairy-processing consolidation even as national milk production keeps growing; useful competitive context.
*Recommended action:* Monitor regional coverage; no action needed. [Dairy Herd Management](https://www.dairyherd.com/news/foremost-farms-close-marshfield-plant-eliminating-68-jobs-mid-december)

**CDC Declares Clover Hill Dairy Listeria Outbreak Over** (Low · Monitor Only) — CDC formally closed the multistate Listeria outbreak linked to Clover Hill Dairy soft cheese, which led to a Class I recall earlier this year; the investigation ended with 12 confirmed illnesses, 10 hospitalizations and one death across four states.
*Why it matters:* Closes the loop on a fatal soft-cheese Listeria outbreak; remains a live reference point for Listeria-control practices at facilities producing soft/fresh cheese.
*Recommended action:* No further action; note closure in internal food-safety tracking. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/listeria/outbreaks/soft-cheese-06-26/index.html)

**U.S. Dairy Processing Investment Has Reached $13 Billion Over the Past Year, NMPF CEO Says** (Low · Monitor Only) — NMPF's CEO said U.S. dairy processors have invested roughly $13 billion in new and expanded manufacturing capacity over the past year, citing expansions from several major processors and flagging longer-term logistics and transportation questions.
*Why it matters:* A sustained, sector-wide capacity build-out reshapes competitive dynamics and the logistics/labor landscape; useful context for capacity and market-share planning.
*Recommended action:* Monitor competitor capacity announcements. [Brownfield Ag News](https://www.brownfieldagnews.com/news/13-billion-in-dairy-processing-investment-reshapes-u-s-industry/)

**Free-Market and Consumer-Rights Groups Urge OMB to Block FDA's Ultra-Processed Food Definition** (Low · Monitor Only) — Sixteen free-market and consumer-rights organizations wrote to the White House budget director urging him to block the pending federal "ultra-processed food" definition, arguing it lacks scientific grounding and would raise grocery costs.
*Why it matters:* Adds a new, ideologically distinct source of pressure against the same OIRA-pending UPF definition already on the watch list, signaling the release timeline and scope remain contested.
*Recommended action:* Continue monitoring the OIRA review; no action needed while this remains advocacy pressure. [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/27/free-market-groups-processed-food-definition)

**HHS Launches $32.5 Million DEPEND Initiative to Fund School Nutrition Pilot Programs** (Low · Monitor Only) — HHS announced a $32.5 million initiative to fund school-district pilot programs testing ways to replace processed foods with nutrient-dense options; first awards are expected in October.
*Why it matters:* A second MAHA-aligned school-nutrition push in three days; the pilot criteria selected this fall could eventually shape school procurement standards.
*Recommended action:* Monitor which pilot approaches are funded in October. [HHS.gov](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-depend-initiative-healthier-school-meals-real-food.html)
