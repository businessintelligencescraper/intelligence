# Corporate Affairs Intelligence — Weekly Briefing — Week of August 17–23, 2026

## The week in one paragraph

The past week turned the GRAS-notification overhaul from a quiet regulatory process into a national news story: HHS Secretary Kennedy publicly unveiled the mandatory-notification proposal on Aug. 10, and FDA formally published it in the Federal Register the next day, opening a 120-day comment window. Alongside it, Bloomberg revealed that food-industry CEOs had been lobbying the White House to delay the parallel ultra-processed-food definition, adding a "who lobbied" thread to that story. On the advocacy front, a bipartisan group of lawmakers and NMPF pressed FDA to enforce dairy standards of identity against plant-based labeling, and dairy farmers notched a court win preserving their EPCRA air-emissions exemption. Trade tension with Canada intensified as negotiators raced toward (but had not reached) a framework deal days ahead of the Aug. 19 tariff deadline. Industry consolidation continued in the background — Saputo's sale of its UK division to Lactalis, Bongards' $135 million cheese-plant expansion, and fresh data showing the US dairy-farm count on track to fall below 20,000 by 2030 — while a GLP-1-driven shift toward high-protein eating continued to reshape grocery baskets.

## Top developments

**HHS Secretary Publicly Announces GRAS Notification Overhaul, Confirms UPF Definition Advancing** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. publicly unveiled FDA's proposed rule requiring mandatory notification for self-affirmed GRAS determinations, ending the voluntary system in place since 1958; HHS and USDA also confirmed a first-ever federal ultra-processed-food definition has been submitted for final review.
*Why it matters:* National outlets (CNN, CNBC) covered this as breaking news, moving a quiet regulatory process into active mainstream coverage that could prompt reporter inquiries to ingredient suppliers and dairy manufacturers. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/10/health/fda-food-ingredients-gras-wellness), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/hhs-makes-announcements-on-gras-ultraprocessed-food.html)

**Food-Industry CEOs Lobbied White House to Delay Ultra-Processed Food Definition** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — Bloomberg reported that food-company CEOs and trade groups sent letters and made calls urging the White House to delay releasing the UPF definition, arguing it would expose companies to litigation and raise costs.
*Why it matters:* Surfaces industry-wide (not company-specific) lobbying against a definition HHS says could underpin future front-of-pack warning labels, creating a "who lobbied" line of questioning leadership should be ready to address. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/ceos-pushed-white-house-to-delay-ultra-processed-food-definition)

**FDA Formally Publishes Mandatory GRAS Notification Proposed Rule in Federal Register** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — FDA filed the mandatory GRAS-notification rule for Federal Register publication Aug. 11 (Document 2026-16296), formally opening a 120-day comment period closing in early December.
*Why it matters:* Starts the comment clock on the biggest change to the GRAS pathway in decades; any dairy ingredient relying on self-affirmed GRAS status is potentially affected, and regulatory/legal teams now have a fixed deadline to prepare a position. [Federal Register](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-16296.pdf), [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/08/11/880938.htm)

**US-Canada Trade Teams Race to Finalize Deal Days Before Aug. 19 Dairy Tariff Deadline** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — With 50% Section 338 tariffs on Canadian-origin whey, milk protein concentrate, lactose, casein and milk powders set for Aug. 19, negotiators reported movement toward a framework deal, though coverage is split on how close it actually is.
*Why it matters:* Direct cost exposure for any dairy manufacturer sourcing Canadian-origin ingredients; whether a deal lands, partially lands, or the tariffs proceed as scheduled will be determined within days. [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/08/15/canada-tariffs-trump-deadline/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/carney-walks-a-political-tightrope-as-us-trade-talks-approach-deadline), [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12022041/canada-us-trade-talks-tariffs-gap-source/)

**Bipartisan Lawmakers and NMPF Press FDA to Enforce Dairy Standards of Identity Against Plant-Based Labeling** (Low · Potential Media Interest) — A bipartisan, bicameral group of lawmakers sent FDA's Acting Commissioner a letter urging enforcement of standards of identity against plant-based products using dairy terms; NMPF publicly reinforced it, calling on FDA to rescind its 2023 voluntary plant-based labeling guidance.
*Why it matters:* Aligns with the company's category interest in protecting standards-of-identity terminology and gives it a ready-made advocacy position to echo if media coverage grows. [NMPF](https://www.nmpf.org/lawmakers-nmpf-press-fda-to-finally-enforce-dairy-standards-of-identity/), [Michigan Farm News](https://www.michiganfarmnews.com/lawmakers-nmpf-press-fda-to-finally-enforce-dairy-standards-of-identity)

**Federal Court Upholds EPA Exemption Shielding Farms From Air-Emissions Reporting** (Low · Monitor Only) — A DC federal court upheld the 2019 EPA rule exempting farm animal-waste air emissions, including dairy operations, from EPCRA reporting; environmental groups are expected to continue a related appeal.
*Why it matters:* Preserves the status quo sparing dairy farms and processors from federal air-emissions reporting on manure byproducts; a reversal on appeal would impose new reporting costs across the supply chain. [NMPF](https://www.nmpf.org/dairy-farmers-near-win-in-decades-long-regulatory-fight/)

**Bicameral Bill Would Set Federal Standards for "Recyclable" and "Compostable" Package Labeling** (Low · Monitor Only) — The TRUTH in Labeling Act of 2026 would direct EPA and FTC to jointly set enforceable federal definitions and a standardized on-pack label for recyclability/compostability claims.
*Why it matters:* Food and beverage packaging routinely carries these claims; a federal standard could eventually replace the fast-growing state-by-state EPR/labeling patchwork the company already tracks, though this is an early-stage introduction. [Packaging Dive](https://www.packagingdive.com/news/truth-in-labeling-act-correa-merkley/827421/), [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/policy-now/2026/08/10/bicameral-truth-in-labeling-act-introduced-in-congress/)

**Bongards' Creameries Announces $135 Million Process Cheese Facility Expansion** (Low · Monitor Only) — The Minnesota dairy co-op will invest $135 million to add roughly 180 million lbs/year of process cheese capacity, one of the largest capital investments in its 118-year history.
*Why it matters:* Signals continued confidence in US process-cheese demand and rising category capacity — useful competitive context for capacity and investment planning. [Dairy Herd](https://www.dairyherd.com/news/bongards-bets-big-135-million-expansion-signals-confidence-process-cheese-future)

**GLP-1 Weight-Loss Drug Use Continues to Reshape Grocery Spending** (Low · Monitor Only) — New Numerator data shows 22% of US households now include a GLP-1 medication user, with those households shifting purchases toward protein and fiber and away from pantry/carb staples.
*Why it matters:* A demand tailwind worth tracking for high-protein dairy categories (Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, protein milk) even as it pressures overall grocery-basket spending. [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/glp1-weight-loss-drugs-medication-grocery-spend-numerator/827405/)

**US Dairy Farm Count on Track to Fall Below 20,000 by 2030, Analysis Finds** (Low · Monitor Only) — Trade-press analysis of USDA data finds the dairy sector on pace for fewer than 20,000 licensed farms by decade's end, down from 131,500 three decades ago, even as total production keeps rising on fewer, larger operations.
*Why it matters:* Accelerating upstream consolidation reshapes the milk-supply base toward fewer, larger operations, with longer-term implications for sourcing strategy and "family farm" messaging. [Dairy Reporter](https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2026/08/07/us-dairy-farm-numbers-set-to-fall-below-20000-amid-industry-consolidation/)

**Saputo Agrees to Sell UK Dairy Division to Lactalis for £988 Million** (Low · Monitor Only) — Saputo will sell its UK Dairy Division — five plants and brands including Cathedral City and Clover — to Lactalis for roughly $1.86 billion, closing expected Q1 calendar 2027.
*Why it matters:* Consolidates two of the sector's largest global processors and signals continued geography-driven portfolio rationalization — a useful data point for competitive-landscape tracking. [Saputo](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/14/3345243/0/en/saputo-enters-agreement-to-sell-its-united-kingdom-operations.html), [Food Manufacture](https://www.foodmanufacture.co.uk/Article/2026/08/14/cathedral-city-and-clover-owner-sells-off-uk-dairy-operations/)

**USDA's August WASDE Report Holds 2026 Milk Production Forecast Flat, Trims 2027 Outlook** (Low · Monitor Only) — USDA left the 2026 milk-production forecast unchanged at 236.6 billion lbs. while trimming 2027; product-price forecasts were mixed, with cheese and dry whey raised and butter and nonfat dry milk lowered.
*Why it matters:* A routine but closely watched monthly signal for the sector's supply and pricing trajectory, relevant context for margin and product-mix planning. [Cheese Reporter](https://cheesereporter.com/news/production-supply-chain/2026/08/14/dairy-product-milk-price-forecasts-mixed-in-latest-usda-projections/)
