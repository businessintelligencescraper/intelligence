# Corporate Affairs Intelligence — Weekly Briefing — Week of September 14–20, 2026

## The week in one paragraph

The dominant theme this week was the US-Canada dairy trade dispute crossing from tariffs into an outright import ban: Canada's retaliatory tariffs on US dairy took effect Sept. 8 (50% on milk and dairy ingredients, 25% on cheese), and the Trump administration escalated the same day, banning imports of Canadian dairy, alcohol and motorcycles effective Sept. 29 with no resumed-talks date announced. On the legislative front, 76 dairy companies, cooperatives and producer groups joined NMPF and IDFA in publicly urging Congress to reject the Childhood Diabetes Reduction Act's ultra-processed-food warning-label mandate — which would apply to whole milk, cheese and most yogurts — ahead of an expected Senate floor vote. Separately, an industry-funded report reviewing two dairy-supply-chain infant-formula botulism outbreaks drew criticism for including no independent or parent voices, a reputational flag for industry-funded food-safety science more broadly. Routine news rounded out the week: two narrow, no-illness dairy recalls (a repeat-offender Long Island deli producer and a mislabeled imported specialty cheese), a trade-press plant-of-the-year honor for an ice cream manufacturer, refreshed IDFA governance leadership, and a fresh EPA comment window on wetlands jurisdiction affecting farm and plant water permitting.

## Top developments

**Trump Administration Bans Imports of Canadian Dairy, Alcohol and Motorcycles in Trade War Escalation** (High · Executive Awareness Required) — President Trump signed proclamations under Section 338 banning imports of certain Canadian dairy products (including whey), most alcoholic beverages, motorcycles and molasses, effective Sept. 29, 2026. The action follows Canada's dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs taking effect Sept. 8 and cites Canada's continued failure to remove what the administration calls discrimination against US dairy commerce after trade talks collapsed in August.
*Why it matters:* This is a step beyond tariffs to an outright import ban, directly restricting the Canadian-origin whey and other dairy ingredients some US processors source, adding fresh supply-chain and sourcing uncertainty on top of the existing 50%/25% tariff regime already in effect. NMPF and IDFA both voiced support for pressure on Canada over USMCA dairy-access commitments while cautioning that a prolonged trade war raises costs and uncertainty for US processors, with no resumed-talks date announced.
*Recommended action:* Brief leadership on the Sept. 29 effective date and confirm any current reliance on Canadian-sourced whey or other affected dairy ingredients before then; watch for a Canadian counter-response.
[The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/excluding-certain-canadian-products-from-importation-into-the-united-states-in-response-to-continued-discrimination-against-the-commerce-of-the-united-states-with-respect-to-dairy/), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/09/08/us-announces-ban-certain-canadian-imports-dairy-alcohol-other-products/), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/economy/trump-escalates-trade-war-canada-moving-ban-import-autos-dairy-alcohol-rcna596666)

**Canada's Retaliatory Tariffs on US Dairy Take Effect, Hitting Milk at 50% and Cheese at 25%** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — Canada's "dollar-for-dollar" retaliatory tariffs on roughly $20–28 billion of US goods took effect at 12:01 a.m. Sept. 8, 2026, after weeks of confirmed lead time. Milk, cream and dairy ingredients (whey, caseinates, lactose) are taxed at 50%; finished cheese at 25% — matching the rates the US had imposed on Canadian dairy, alcohol and auto imports under Section 338/232 tariffs after talks collapsed Aug. 22.
*Why it matters:* This is the actual go-live of tariffs this system has tracked since Aug. 22 — US dairy and dairy-ingredient exporters to Canada now face materially higher landed costs with no resumed-talks date announced, and ingredient exporters (whey, caseinates, lactose) face a steeper 50% rate than finished cheese at 25%.
*Recommended action:* Confirm current Canada-bound export exposure at the now-effective 50%/25% rates; watch for any sign of resumed US-Canada talks before assuming this is the settled state.
[CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/canada-retaliatory-tariffs.html), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/canada-retaliatory-tariffs-us-goods-milk-perfume-golf-clubs-trump/)

**76 Dairy Companies and Co-ops Join NMPF, IDFA in Urging Congress to Reject Ultra-Processed Warning-Label Bill** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — The National Milk Producers Federation and International Dairy Foods Association, joined by 76 dairy companies, cooperatives and state and regional producer organizations, sent congressional leaders a letter urging rejection of S. 5026, the Childhood Diabetes Reduction Act of 2026. The bill would create a broad "ultra-processed foods" definition and require front-of-package warning labels on foods it covers, including whole milk, cheese and most yogurts. NMPF President and CEO Gregg Doud said the bill "gets the science wrong, lumping wholesome dairy foods in with products that have nothing in common with them nutritionally."
*Why it matters:* S. 5026 already awaits a Senate floor vote (tracked on the watch list) and would apply blanket UPF warning labels to nutrient-dense dairy staples; this coalition letter shows the scale of industry mobilization against it just ahead of that vote, raising the visibility of the messaging fight and giving the company a clear industry position to weigh alignment with.
*Recommended action:* Brief leadership on the coalition letter and confirm the company's own positioning relative to NMPF/IDFA messaging before any Senate floor vote on S. 5026.
[Cowsmo](https://cowsmo.com/news/u-s-dairy-companies-cooperatives-and-farmer-organizations-urge-congress-to-reject-s-5026/), [EDairy News](https://en.edairynews.com/us-dairy-sector-urges-congress-to-reject-s-5026/)

**Industry-Funded Report on Infant Formula Botulism Outbreaks Draws Criticism Over Lack of Mandatory Rules** (Medium · Messaging Review Required) — The Reagan-Udall Foundation for the FDA published "Applying the Science of Infant Formula Safety," a report from a July 13 roundtable on Clostridium botulinum risk in powdered infant formula that was funded and co-prepared by ByHeart Inc. and the International Dairy Foods Association (IDFA). The report offers 22 recommendations on testing, data-sharing and supply-chain controls but sets no mandatory requirements; critics note no parent or patient advocate participated in the roundtable.
*Why it matters:* IDFA, the dairy industry's primary trade association, funded and helped prepare the report, and the ingredient at the center of the underlying outbreaks (organic powdered whole milk supplied by Organic West Milk and processed by Dairy Farmers of America) sits squarely in the dairy supply chain. Renewed coverage framing this as industry policing itself carries reputational exposure for dairy trade groups and ingredient suppliers even where a given manufacturer is not implicated.
*Recommended action:* Brief leadership on IDFA's role and prepare a short holding statement in case of press inquiries about industry-funded safety reviews; no proactive outreach needed.
[Reagan-Udall Foundation](https://reaganudall.org/publications/applying-science-infant-formula-safety), [Marler Blog](https://www.marlerblog.com/case-news/byheart-and-the-dairy-industry-paid-for-a-report-on-the-formula-botulism-outbreaks-it-has-22-recommendations-no-parent-in-the-room-and-no-rule/)

**EPA and Army Publish Supplemental WOTUS Proposal, Opening 30-Day Comment Period** (Low · Monitor Only) — EPA and the Army Corps of Engineers published a Supplemental Notice of Proposed Rulemaking in the Federal Register on Sept. 9, 2026, seeking further public input on how to define "waters of the United States" under the Clean Water Act, including the "relatively permanent" waters and "continuous surface water connection" tests. Comments are due by Oct. 9, 2026.
*Why it matters:* WOTUS jurisdiction determines which on-farm and processing-facility waters and wetlands require Clean Water Act permits, so dairy farms and plants with on-site water features have a fresh, now firmly dated window to weigh in before the agencies finalize the rule.
*Recommended action:* Monitor; note the Oct. 9 comment deadline for any environmental/regulatory affairs team considering a submission.
[Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/09/2026-18317/updated-definition-of-waters-of-the-united-states), [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-and-army-seek-additional-input-proposed-waters-us-definition-while-advancing)

**Long Island Deli Producer Recalls All Cream Cheese and Deli Salads for Listeria — Third Recall at the Plant in a Year** (Low · Monitor Only) — Made Fresh Salads, Inc. of Bay Shore, NY recalled every ready-to-eat deli-style salad and cream cheese product it makes on Sept. 6, 2026, after FDA and company environmental sampling found Listeria monocytogenes in the production facility. No illnesses have been reported; it is the plant's third Listeria-related recall in under a year.
*Why it matters:* Cream cheese is a dairy-category product, and a repeat-offender facility drawing renewed FDA scrutiny keeps Listeria in the food-safety news cycle even though this recall is confined to one small regional producer with no national branded dairy manufacturer involved.
*Recommended action:* Monitor for any expansion of the recall or linked illnesses; no direct action needed given the isolated regional scope.
[FDA](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/made-fresh-salads-inc-recalls-ready-eat-deli-style-salads-and-cream-cheese-because-possible-health), [Food Poison Journal](https://www.foodpoisonjournal.com/food-poisoning-information/made-fresh-salads-recalls-every-deli-salad-and-cream-cheese-it-makes-for-listeria-the-third-recall-from-this-plant-in-under-a-year/)

No illnesses have been reported.
*Why it matters:* A narrow, single-retailer allergen-labeling lapse on an imported specialty cheese with no dairy-processor involvement; included as a routine reminder that allergen-declaration diligence on specialty/soft cheese remains an active recall category.
*Recommended action:* No action needed; monitor for any expansion or illness reports.
[FDA](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/whole-foods-market-issues-allergy-alert-undeclared-egg-cabricharme-cheese), [Food Poisoning Bulletin](https://foodpoisoningbulletin.com/2026/whole-foods-cabricharme-cheese-recalled-for-undeclared-egg/)
