# Corporate Affairs Intelligence — Weekly Briefing — Week of October 12–18, 2026

The most consequential thread this week was on the regulatory side: reporting that the White House has quietly slowed several Make America Healthy Again initiatives, including the planned federal definition of "ultra-processed food" and a revamp of school-meal rules, over concerns about litigation exposure and trade effects — a sign the administration is not fully unified on scope even as the underlying science kept accumulating, with a new Virginia Tech brain-imaging study showing ultra-processed meals trigger measurably different metabolic and reward responses than matched whole-food meals. ## Top developments

**White House Reportedly Slowing MAHA's Ultra-Processed-Food Definition and School-Meal Rule Changes** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required)
The Wall Street Journal reported, relayed Oct. 5 by The Food Institute, that the White House has slowed several Make America Healthy Again initiatives led by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., including the planned federal definition of "ultra-processed food" and a revamp of USDA school-meal guidelines, over concerns about exposure in ongoing food-related litigation and effects on international trade. *Why it matters:* A federal UPF definition is the building block regulators would need before any front-of-pack warning label or category-wide marketing restriction could reach packaged dairy products like flavored milk, yogurt and cheese snacks; internal administration friction could push that timeline further out, and the lack of unity across agencies is useful context for any public engagement on the definition. [The Food Institute](https://foodinstitute.com/healthwellness/maha-healthier-food-campaign-stalled-white-house/)

**Virginia Tech Study Finds Ultra-Processed Meals Trigger Different Metabolic and Brain-Reward Responses Than Matched Whole-Food Meals** (Medium · Potential Media Interest)
A controlled-feeding and brain-imaging study published in Nature Metabolism on Oct. 5 found that ultra-processed and minimally processed meals, matched within 1% for calories and macronutrients, still produced measurably different effects — more insulin release and different brain dopaminergic-reward activation — in the same 57 adults. *Why it matters:* This is a rigorously controlled study, not an observational one, designed specifically to show that processing itself changes metabolic and brain-reward responses independent of nutrient content. It doesn't implicate dairy directly but strengthens the general UPF narrative feeding the pending federal definition effort, which would eventually reach processed dairy formats. [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-calories-responses-food-metabolism-brain.html), [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/ultraprocessed-food-changes-metabolic-response-study-12523675)

**AAP Renews Call to Ban Raw Milk Sales, Citing New H5N1 Risk** (Medium · Potential Media Interest)
The American Academy of Pediatrics published an updated clinical report renewing its call for a ban on raw milk sales, citing established Listeria/E. coli/Salmonella/Campylobacter/Brucella risks plus new evidence that H5N1 avian influenza can reach high concentrations in raw milk and stay detectable for up to five weeks under refrigeration. *Why it matters:* Pasteurized dairy manufacturers are not implicated, but renewed advocacy from a major pediatric body, amplified by national press and a novel H5N1 angle, keeps "dairy and illness" headlines circulating and could prompt fresh state legislative attention to raw-milk sales rules, with some category-level reputation spillover risk. [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wellness/2026/10/03/raw-milk-is-dangerous-pediatricians-warn/), [Dairy Reporter](https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2026/10/06/renewed-call-for-raw-dairy-ban-amid-h5n1-concerns/)

**Consumer Class Action Accuses Dairy Cooperatives' Export Program of Price-Fixing** (Medium · Monitor Only)
A proposed federal consumer class action filed in the Eastern District of Virginia accuses the National Milk Producers Federation and 14 major dairy marketing cooperatives — representing roughly 70% of U.S. milk production — of using their NEXT export-assistance program to restrict domestic raw milk supply and inflate prices, alleging Sherman Act and state antitrust violations. *Why it matters:* This extends a decade-plus string of antitrust suits against dairy cooperative supply-management programs, several of which ended in multi-million-dollar settlements; it is an early-stage filing with no ruling yet, but any manufacturer sourcing milk through NEXT-participating cooperatives should watch for discovery findings or class certification. [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/consumers-accuse-milk-cartel-of-antitrust-violations/), [Feedstuffs](https://www.feedstuffs.com/agribusiness-news/consumer-antitrust-suit-accuses-dairy-co-ops-of-price-fixing)

**IDFA Warns EU Corporate Due-Diligence Rules Are Raising Costs for U.S. Dairy Exporters** (Low · Monitor Only)
IDFA told U.S. trade officials that the EU's Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive and related supply-chain rules are imposing significant compliance costs on U.S. dairy processors and farmers, including some that don't sell directly into Europe, with members reporting a full social-audit cycle approaching $27,000 per site and over 100 staff hours. *Why it matters:* Companies without direct EU sales can still be pulled into compliance costs through EU-facing customers or co-packers, making this a cost and resourcing issue for the broader U.S. dairy sector; IDFA is pressing for a U.S. exemption from the rule's extraterritorial reach, which is the lever worth watching. [Dairy Foods](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/99437-idfa-eu-due-diligence-rules-are-increasing-costs-for-us-dairy), [Morning Ag Clips](https://www.morningagclips.com/idfa-warns-eu-due-diligence-rules-are-increasing-costs-for-us-dairy/)

**USDA's Dairy Forward Pricing Program Lapses Again as Farm Bill Remains Stalled** (Low · Monitor Only)
USDA's authority for milk handlers to enter new Dairy Forward Pricing Program contracts expired Sept. 30 because Congress has not enacted a new farm bill or other extension; USDA cannot authorize new forward-pricing contracts until that authority is restored, though contracts already in place through 2029 are unaffected. *Why it matters:* This removes a risk-management tool for locking in forward milk prices until Congress acts — a second, distinct consequence of the stalled farm bill beyond the broader program lapse already on the watch list. [Cheese Reporter](https://cheesereporter.com/news/policy-legislation/2026/10/02/usdas-dairy-forward-pricing-program-expires-again/)
