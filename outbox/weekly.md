# Corporate Affairs Intelligence — Weekly Briefing — Week of October 26–November 1, 2026

## The week in one paragraph

Last week's biggest development for the category was the first direct court challenge to the Section 338 tariffs that have been reshaping Canadian dairy-ingredient sourcing costs since summer: a New Jersey cheese importer sued to block the 50% duties, making the Court of International Trade docket the key thing to watch for the tariff outlook. Food safety stayed in the news as FDA formally classified the Sierra Nevada Graziers raw-milk cheese recall as Class I, its most serious category, reinforcing an outbreak story already on leadership's radar. On the packaging-litigation front, Colorado moved to narrow (not end) a wholesalers' constitutional challenge to its EPR law, and a second Oregon EPR suit was paused to let the pending Ninth Circuit appeal run its course first — both continuing a now-familiar multistate legal pattern with no change to current compliance obligations. On a more positive note, IDFA's updated investment tracker put planned U.S. dairy-processing capacity spending above $15 billion across 23 states, a useful data point for growth and jobs messaging.

## Top developments

**New Jersey Cheese Importer Sues to Block 50% Section 338 Tariffs on Canadian Goods** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required)
Gellert Global Group, a $1.7B food importer specializing in Canadian cheese, filed the first direct legal challenge to the Section 338 tariffs on Oct. 20, arguing the administration skipped required procedure and that the 50% rate isn't tied to any measured trade burden; the suit seeks to strike the tariffs and refund duties collected since summer. *Why it matters:* This is now the key swing factor in the tariff/sourcing-cost outlook the industry has been navigating since the Canada trade dispute began — a plaintiff win could unwind the duties and trigger refunds. *Recommended action:* Leadership has been briefed that the tariffs are under direct legal challenge; monitor the Court of International Trade docket for a preliminary-injunction motion or scheduling order. [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bgov.com/international-trade/trump-administration-sued-over-50-tariffs-on-canadian-products), [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/trumps-50-canada-tariffs-challenged-in-court/833495/)

**FDA Assigns Highest-Risk Class I Status to Sierra Nevada Graziers Raw-Milk Cheese Recall** (Medium · Potential Media Interest)
FDA formally classified the September raw-milk cheese recall as Class I as the multistate E. coli outbreak tied to the product held at 13 confirmed illnesses across nine states, with 8 hospitalizations and 3 cases of a serious kidney complication, more than half in children age 5 or younger. *Why it matters:* This keeps raw-milk cheese safety in the news cycle and reinforces the case advocates are making for category-wide warning labeling, though it adds no new facts beyond what was already tracked. [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/cheese-recall-states-highest-warning-issued-e-coli-12592463)

**U.S. Dairy Processors Plan Over $15 Billion in New Processing Capacity Across 23 States** (Low · Monitor Only)
IDFA's updated investment tracker, released during National Manufacturing Month, counts more than 85 planned or underway dairy-processing projects worth over $15 billion — up from roughly $13 billion/19 states in late August — led by New York, Texas and Wisconsin. *Why it matters:* A positive, durable reference point on industry confidence and capacity growth that comms teams may want on hand if the topic comes up. [DairyReporter](https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2026/10/25/us-dairy-industry-investing-15bn-in-new-processing-capacity-across-23-states/)

**Colorado Moves to Partly Dismiss Wholesalers' Challenge to Its Packaging EPR Law** (Low · Monitor Only)
Colorado's health department filed a motion to dismiss two of the constitutional claims in a wholesaler trade group's lawsuit over the state's packaging EPR law, leaving the remaining claims and a separate injunction motion untouched. *Why it matters:* A partial state win would narrow but not resolve the litigation overhang facing all seven states with packaging EPR laws, several of which cover dairy packaging; no change to current obligations. [Foley & Lardner](https://www.foley.com/?p=130154)

**Second Oregon Packaging-EPR Suit (Lollicup) Paused Pending Ninth Circuit Appeal** (Low · Monitor Only)
A second Oregon EPR challenge was paused by joint agreement to let the related Ninth Circuit appeal — now the controlling test case for Oregon-style packaging EPR laws — run its course first. *Why it matters:* Confirms the appeal is the vehicle to watch; no change to current Oregon EPR compliance obligations. [Packaging Dive](https://www.packagingdive.com/news/lollicup-oregon-class-action-stay-pending-naw-appeal/832828/)
