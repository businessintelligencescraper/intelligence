**First US Bird Flu Death of 2026 Confirmed in Washington, Linked to Dairy Cattle Exposure**
The Yakima Health District confirmed that a Yakima County, Washington resident with underlying health conditions died Nov. 1, 2026 after contracting H5N1 avian influenza following exposure to infected dairy cattle — the first US human death from bird flu in 2026 and the third nationally since dairy-cattle H5N1 was first detected in March 2024. CDC says its genetic analysis of the virus found no mutations indicating increased transmissibility or severity, and state and local health officials say the risk to the general public remains low; Yakima County has recorded more than 30 H5N1-positive dairy herds since early October.
*Why it matters:* National wire and broadcast coverage is running headlines pairing "dairy cattle" with a human death just weeks after Washington recorded its first-ever dairy-herd H5N1 detections. Officials stress that pasteurized milk remains safe and that the risk is isolated to farm workers with direct animal exposure, but the dairy category should expect renewed consumer questions about milk safety and media requests for comment on biosecurity and worker-protection measures.
*Recommended action:* Brief executive and communications leadership now; have pasteurization-safety and farm-biosecurity talking points ready for media inquiries, and confirm whether any sourcing ties exist to Yakima-area herds.
