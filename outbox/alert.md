# ⚠ High-Risk Alert — August 6, 2026

**FDA Assigns Highest-Risk Classification to Fatal Clover Hill Dairy Cheese Listeria Recall**
The FDA on Wednesday, August 5 assigned a Class I risk classification — its highest — to the recall of Clover Hill Dairy's cheese products, made at the company's Mechanicsville, Maryland facility. The recall, which began in June 2026 with the company's soft ricotta/requeson line and was expanded to all of its cheese products after Listeria monocytogenes contamination was confirmed, has been linked to 9 illnesses, 8 hospitalizations, and 1 death across Maryland, New York, and Virginia; over 29,200 lbs of cheese were distributed in bulk and at retail, including under relabeled brand names such as Kesso, Quesos La Ricura, Izalco, De Mi Pueblo, and Rio Lindo.
*Why it matters:* A Class I classification — FDA's determination that a product carries a reasonable probability of causing serious illness or death — on a fatal Listeria outbreak traced to a dairy cheese manufacturer is a direct, live case study in the category-wide reputational and regulatory exposure a soft-cheese contamination event can carry; it's a prompt to review environmental-monitoring and Listeria-control programs at any facility producing soft/fresh cheeses, particularly given FDA traced the outbreak strain back to illness onset as early as 2023.
*Recommended action:* Brief food-safety/quality leadership on the Class I classification; use as a prompt to review environmental-monitoring and Listeria-control protocols for any soft-cheese production lines; monitor for further CDC/FDA outbreak updates.
