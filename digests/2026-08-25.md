## ⚠ Priority items

**Canada Publishes Retaliatory Tariff List: US Dairy and Cheese Exports Face 25% Duties Starting Sept. 8** — Canada published the full product list and rates for its "dollar for dollar" retaliatory tariffs on Aug. 25, covering more than 700 US goods worth roughly $20 billion. Dairy and cheese products are among the categories hit with a 25% duty (alongside appliances and other goods), steel and aluminum face 50%, and most remaining items are set at 15%; the tariffs take effect Sept. 8. PM Mark Carney and Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc confirmed the list after talks with Washington collapsed and US tariffs on Canadian dairy ingredients took effect Aug. 22.
*Why it matters:* This is the first confirmed, quantified rate for the dairy-sector retaliation Canada flagged on Aug. 22 — a 25% tariff on US dairy and cheese exports to Canada, effective in under two weeks. It directly raises costs and reduces competitiveness for any US dairy manufacturer exporting cheese or dairy products north of the border, and it means tariffs are now elevated in both directions between the two countries with no resumed-talks timeline announced.
*Recommended action:* Brief leadership on the confirmed 25% tariff on US dairy/cheese exports to Canada ahead of the Sept. 8 effective date; assess Canada-bound export exposure and pricing implications now that specific rates are public.

### Dairy Industry

**Dairy Industry Completes Pledge to Remove Artificial Colors From School Milk, Cheese and Yogurt** (Low · Potential Media Interest) — America's dairy processors have fulfilled IDFA's voluntary "Healthy Dairy in Schools Commitment," eliminating seven certified artificial colors (Red 3, Red 40, Green 3, Blue 1, Blue 2, Yellow 5, Yellow 6) from milk, cheese and yogurt sold into the National School Lunch and School Breakfast Programs, ahead of the 2026-2027 school year. It is the second consecutive year the industry has delivered on a voluntary school-nutrition pledge, following last year's reduction of added sugar in flavored school milk.
*Why it matters:* A positive, verifiable industry-wide compliance story that surpasses state and federal standards and gives dairy manufacturers a concrete talking point amid ongoing public and legislative scrutiny of artificial food dyes; likely to draw trade-press and possibly general-media pickup given the national debate over food dyes in schools. [Dairy Foods](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/99348-dairy-industry-meets-commitment-to-remove-artificial-colors-from-school-dairy-products), [GlobeNewswire (IDFA release, via The Manila Times)](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/08/25/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/a-promise-made-a-promise-kept-americas-dairy-industry-delivers-on-second-school-nutrition-commitment/2411974)

### Sustainability

**Bipartisan Senate Bill Would Fund Dairy Manure-Management Upgrades** (Low · Monitor Only) — Sen. Alex Padilla (D-CA) reintroduced the Converting Our Waste Sustainably (COWS) Act, with Sen. Martin Heinrich (D-NM) signing on as a cosponsor in mid-August. The bill would create a voluntary USDA grant program, built on the existing Environmental Quality Incentives Program, to help dairy and other livestock producers install alternative manure-management systems that cut methane emissions and improve water and air quality.
*Why it matters:* Manure management and methane reduction are recurring themes in dairy sustainability reporting and farmer-supplier engagement; a funded voluntary federal program would give the company's farmer-suppliers a cost-effective emissions-reduction pathway without new mandates. The bill has not been scheduled for committee markup and is one of several farm-sustainability measures competing for floor time this Congress, so near-term impact is limited. [Sen. Martin Heinrich (Senate.gov)](https://www.heinrich.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/heinrich-cosponsors-cows-act-to-reduce-greenhouse-emissions-support-sustainable-waste-practices), [New Mexico Political Report](https://nmpoliticalreport.com/2026/08/23/money-for-manure-heinrich-joins-congressional-effort-to-pay-farmers-to-manage-mountains-of-manure/)
