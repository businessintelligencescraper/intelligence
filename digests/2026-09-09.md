## ⚠ Priority items

**Trump Administration Bans Imports of Canadian Dairy, Alcohol and Motorcycles in Trade War Escalation** — President Trump signed proclamations under Section 338 banning imports of certain Canadian dairy products (including whey), most alcoholic beverages, motorcycles and molasses, effective Sept. 29, 2026. The action follows Canada's dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs taking effect Sept. 8 and cites Canada's continued failure to remove what the administration calls discrimination against US dairy commerce after trade talks collapsed in August. *Why it matters:* This is a step beyond tariffs to an outright import ban, directly restricting the Canadian-origin whey and other dairy ingredients some US processors source, adding fresh supply-chain and sourcing uncertainty on top of the existing 50%/25% tariff regime already in effect. NMPF and IDFA both voiced support for pressure on Canada over USMCA dairy-access commitments while cautioning that a prolonged trade war raises costs and uncertainty for US processors, with no resumed-talks date announced. *Recommended action:* Brief leadership on the Sept. 29 effective date and confirm any current reliance on Canadian-sourced whey or other affected dairy ingredients before then; watch for a Canadian counter-response. **Industry-Funded Report on Infant Formula Botulism Outbreaks Draws Criticism Over Lack of Mandatory Rules** — The Reagan-Udall Foundation for the FDA published "Applying the Science of Infant Formula Safety," a report from a July 13 roundtable on Clostridium botulinum risk in powdered infant formula that was funded and co-prepared by ByHeart Inc. and the International Dairy Foods Association (IDFA). The report offers 22 recommendations on testing, data-sharing and supply-chain controls but sets no mandatory requirements; critics note no parent or patient advocate participated in the roundtable. *Why it matters:* IDFA, the dairy industry's primary trade association, funded and helped prepare the report, and the ingredient at the center of the underlying outbreaks (organic powdered whole milk supplied by Organic West Milk and processed by Dairy Farmers of America) sits squarely in the dairy supply chain. Renewed coverage framing this as industry policing itself carries reputational exposure for dairy trade groups and ingredient suppliers even where a given manufacturer is not implicated. *Recommended action:* Brief leadership on IDFA's role and prepare a short holding statement in case of press inquiries about industry-funded safety reviews; no proactive outreach needed. [Reagan-Udall Foundation](https://reaganudall.org/publications/applying-science-infant-formula-safety), [Food Safety Magazine](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/11801-recommendations-from-industry-led-reagan-udall-roundtable-on-controlling-c-botulinum-in-infant-formula), [Marler Blog](https://www.marlerblog.com/case-news/byheart-and-the-dairy-industry-paid-for-a-report-on-the-formula-botulism-outbreaks-it-has-22-recommendations-no-parent-in-the-room-and-no-rule/)
