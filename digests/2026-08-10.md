# Corporate Affairs Intelligence — Daily Brief — August 10, 2026

## ⚠ Priority items

**HHS Secretary Publicly Announces GRAS Notification Overhaul, Confirms UPF Definition Advancing** — HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. publicly announced FDA's proposed rule requiring mandatory notification when manufacturers self-determine a food ingredient is "Generally Recognized as Safe" (GRAS), ending the voluntary-only system in place since 1958; Federal Register publication remains on track for December 2026. HHS and USDA also confirmed the government's first proposed ultra-processed food definition has been submitted for final review, though its content hasn't been released. *Why it matters:* This is the public unveiling of a policy already on the watch list after it cleared OMB review in July — CNN, CNBC, AP and other national outlets covered it today, moving a quiet regulatory-process story into active mainstream news that could prompt press outreach to ingredient suppliers and dairy manufacturers. The parallel UPF-definition effort is the building block HHS has said could underpin a future front-of-pack labeling system. *Recommended action:* Note the elevated media visibility; no change yet to the December comment-period timeline. Be prepared for reporter inquiries referencing today's coverage. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/10/health/fda-food-ingredients-gras-wellness), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/hhs-makes-announcements-on-gras-ultraprocessed-food.html)

**Food-Industry CEOs Lobbied White House to Delay Ultra-Processed Food Definition** — Bloomberg reported that food-company chief executives and trade group representatives sent letters and made calls to the White House in recent weeks urging it to delay release of the federal government's first proposed definition of ultra-processed foods, arguing that publishing it would expose companies to litigation, raise costs and hurt business. The report follows HHS and USDA's Aug. 10 confirmation that the UPF-definition whitepaper has been submitted for final government (OIRA) review, with no release date set. *Why it matters:* This confirms industry-wide, not company-specific, resistance to a UPF definition that HHS has said could become the basis for front-of-pack warning labeling; the disclosure of coordinated CEO lobbying creates a "who lobbied" line of reputational questioning that leadership and government-affairs teams should be ready to address if the company or its trade associations are named or asked to confirm involvement. *Recommended action:* Brief executive leadership and government-affairs teams that this lobbying activity has surfaced in national press; align messaging with trade-association posture in case of media inquiries about industry lobbying on the UPF definition. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/ceos-pushed-white-house-to-delay-ultra-processed-food-definition)

### Food Industry

**USDA FSIS Issues Public Health Alert for Meat and Poultry Products Containing Recalled Jalapeños** (Low · Monitor Only) — FSIS on Aug. 8 issued a public health alert covering roughly 18 meat and poultry products — sold under retailer brands including Walmart's Marketside, Kroger, H-E-B, Albertsons, Randalls, Tom Thumb, Hannaford, RaceTrac and Wawa across more than 20 states — made with FDA-regulated jalapeños tied to the Salmonella outbreak traced to Coast Citrus Distributors' Sinaloa, Mexico-grown peppers. No illnesses have been confirmed from the FSIS-regulated products themselves; the underlying outbreak has sickened 345 people across 27 states. *Why it matters:* Not a dairy product and no company exposure, but the alert illustrates how a single contaminated produce ingredient can cascade into recalls across multiple manufacturers, retailers and food categories — a useful reference point for any packaged-food producer's ingredient-traceability and supplier-verification programs. [USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/recalls-alerts/fsis-issues-public-health-alert-various-meat-and-poultry-products-containing-fda-0), [WGCU News](https://www.wgcu.org/health/2026-08-09/public-health-alert-for-various-meat-and-poultry-products-containing-recalled-jalapenos)
