## New developments

### Regulatory

**FDA Proposes First-Phase Update to Infant Formula Nutrient Requirements** (Low · Monitor Only) — FDA published a proposed rule on Nov. 2 (announced Oct. 29) that would revise iodine, manganese, and vitamin A and D requirements for infant formula and reaffirm existing limits for chloride, potassium, selenium and sodium — the first of three planned phases updating the required nutrients for infant formula, part of the administration's "Operation Stork Speed" initiative. It would also change the units of measure for vitamin A and D labeling; industry analysis says the unit-conversion requirement would trigger relabeling of all infant-formula UPCs regardless of whether nutrient levels change. FDA says the update is not safety-driven and that formulas on the market remain safe.
*Why it matters:* Opens the first phase of a multi-year overhaul of infant formula composition standards — the first update in decades — that dairy-ingredient suppliers into the infant formula category (lactose, milk protein concentrates, whey) should begin tracking, even though this phase's specific nutrients and the mandatory relabeling fall on formula manufacturers rather than ingredient suppliers; later phases are expected to reach carbohydrate sources and the remaining required nutrients. [Federal Register](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-22273.pdf), [Keller and Heckman LLP](https://www.khlaw.com/fda-proposes-rule-update-infant-formula-nutrient-requirements)
