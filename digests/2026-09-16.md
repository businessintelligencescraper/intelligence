**DOJ Settles Contaminated Infant-Formula Case With Abbott for $385M, Drops Criminal Charges Amid Political-Donation Scrutiny** — The Justice Department announced Sept. 14, 2026 that Abbott Laboratories will pay nearly $385 million to settle False Claims Act allegations that it concealed bacterial-contamination findings at its Sturgis, Michigan infant-formula plant, whose 2022 shutdown (tied to Cronobacter sakazakii contamination and several infant illnesses and deaths) triggered a nationwide formula shortage. DOJ leadership closed a parallel criminal investigation in favor of the civil settlement, a more lenient outcome than career prosecutors reportedly believed the evidence supported; the decision has drawn scrutiny after reporting that Abbott donated $500,000 to President Trump's inaugural fund and that Trump later purchased Abbott stock. *Why it matters:* Abbott is an infant-formula maker, not a dairy processor, but this is a live case study in how the current DOJ is exercising discretion on food-safety criminal enforcement, and the political-donation narrative now attached to that decision, that any food manufacturer should expect to shape enforcement expectations and media framing going forward. A New York state senator has already cited the settlement to press Gov. Hochul to sign the state's pending infant-formula heavy-metal disclosure bill. *Recommended action:* Brief food-safety/regulatory-affairs leadership on DOJ's enforcement posture in this case and the political scrutiny attached to it; no direct action needed, but monitor for spillover into broader food-safety-enforcement coverage.
