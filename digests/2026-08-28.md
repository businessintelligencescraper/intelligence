## ⚠ Priority items

**Federal Judge Upholds Oregon's Packaging EPR Law as Constitutional** — In a 71-page ruling issued Aug. 27, U.S. District Judge Michael H. Simon rejected the National Association of Wholesaler-Distributors' challenge to Oregon's Plastic Pollution and Recycling Modernization Act, finding the state's extended producer responsibility (EPR) packaging law does not violate the Dormant Commerce Clause or Due Process Clause. The ruling followed a five-day bench trial in July and was the first full federal trial to test an EPR packaging law's constitutionality on a complete evidentiary record.
*Why it matters:* This was the bellwether case for all seven states with packaging EPR laws (CA, CO, ME, MD, MN, OR, WA); upholding it makes similar constitutional challenges elsewhere harder to win, reinforcing that packaging-EPR compliance costs facing packaged dairy manufacturers are likely durable rather than at near-term legal risk. NAW is expected to appeal.
*Recommended action:* Brief packaging/sustainability leadership that Oregon's EPR law was upheld and litigation risk to EPR compliance costs nationally has decreased for now; watch for a Ninth Circuit appeal.
