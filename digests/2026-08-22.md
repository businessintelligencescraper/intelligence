**US-Canada Trade Talks Collapse; 50% Tariffs on Canadian Dairy Ingredients Take Effect** — Talks between the US and Canada collapsed just before the extended deadline; Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney suspended negotiations Friday evening, Aug. 21, and recalled Canada's negotiators to Ottawa, saying Canada will match the US tariffs "dollar for dollar." The 50% Section 338 tariffs on Canadian-origin whey, milk protein concentrates, lactose, casein/caseinates and milk powders (alongside alcohol and autos) took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET Saturday, Aug. 22, as originally proclaimed; cheese, butter, yogurt, fluid milk/cream, sour cream and ice cream remain exempt.
*Why it matters:* This resolves weeks of on-again, off-again negotiation in the least favorable direction for the industry: the tariff is now live on Canadian-origin dairy ingredient imports, an immediate cost increase for any US dairy manufacturer sourcing whey, milk protein concentrate, lactose, casein or milk powder from Canada. Canada's promised dollar-for-dollar retaliation raises the additional prospect of new Canadian duties on US dairy exports, with no signed deal or resumed-talks timeline yet in view.
*Recommended action:* Brief leadership that the tariff is now in effect; confirm procurement/finance readiness for the added cost on Canadian-origin whey, milk protein concentrate, lactose, casein and milk powder; watch for Canada's retaliatory tariff list for any impact on US dairy exports.

### Dairy Industry

**First New World Screwworm Case Confirmed in Sonora Days Before Scheduled Cattle-Border Reopening; USDA Proceeds Anyway** (Medium · Potential Media Interest) — Mexico confirmed its first New World screwworm case in the state of Sonora on Aug. 19, in a cow about 292 miles from the U.S. border; Sonora released 360,000 sterile flies around the detection site and stepped up surveillance. Despite the case, USDA said Aug. 21 it has no plans to change the scheduled Aug. 24 reopening of the Douglas, Arizona port to Mexican cattle imports, the first phased reopening since the border was closed in May 2025. *Why it matters:* This is exactly the contingency flagged when the reopening was announced: a new screwworm detection near the pipeline supplying US cattle, including dairy replacement heifers. USDA holding the line on schedule is a near-term positive for herd-supply normalization, but the case keeps the reopening one detection away from reversal and is starting to draw broader media attention about rancher unease.
