**US Import Ban on Canadian Dairy, Alcohol and Motorcycles Takes Effect as Scheduled** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — The Section 338 import ban President Trump proclaimed Sept. 8, 2026 took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET Sept. 29, barring importation of Canadian-origin whey protein concentrates, dried whey, fluid whey, modified whey and other affected dairy products, alongside most alcoholic beverages, motorcycles and molasses, with no USMCA exemption. No last-minute deal or Canadian counter-action was announced before the deadline; Canadian whey processors had already reported stockpiling inventory and struggling to find substitutes in the days beforehand.
*Why it matters:* This is the scheduled, previously-flagged implementation of a ban US processors were already briefed on when it was announced Sept. 8 — it removes Canadian-origin whey as a sourcing option outright, compounding the 50% Section 338 tariff already in effect on other Canadian dairy ingredients since Aug. 22 and Canada's own retaliatory tariffs on US dairy exports since Sept. 8. No resolution timeline has been announced.

**Food-Safety Attorney Opens Public Comment on Draft FDA Petition for Raw Milk Cheese Warning Labels** (Medium · Monitor Only) — Food-safety law firm Marler Clark opened a public comment period on a 22-page draft citizen petition asking FDA to require a warning statement on all cheese made from unpasteurized milk, following the E. coli O26 outbreak linked to Sierra Nevada Cheese Co.'s Graziers raw milk cheese that has sickened 13 people in nine states. Attorney Bill Marler is soliciting feedback from cheesemakers, retailers, public health officials and consumers through October 9, 2026 before filing the petition with FDA, which would then have 180 days to respond.
*Why it matters:* A formal FDA petition seeking a mandatory warning label on an entire product category (aged raw-milk cheese) would be a new labeling precedent and keeps the recent outbreak in the news cycle; any dairy processor that sells aged raw-milk cheese should track the comment window and the eventual FDA docket once filed.

**US Dairy Groups Press Newly Launched US-China Board of Trade to Fully Lift Retaliatory Tariffs** (Medium · Monitor Only) — NMPF and the U.S. Dairy Export Council issued statements Sept. 28 welcoming the launch of the U.S.-China Board of Trade, created alongside the broader US-China tariff truce that was extended that week to Jan. 10, 2027. Both groups said dairy has been included among products slated for retaliatory-tariff review and urged the Board to move quickly to eliminate the tariffs China still applies to US dairy exports.
*Why it matters:* Chinese retaliatory tariffs have sharply reduced US dairy competitiveness there — whey previously made up roughly half of China's dairy import volume — so a Board mandate that actually removes them would reopen a major export market; the industry's own statement makes clear relief is not yet secured, and the groups separately flagged that proposed port fees on Chinese ships must not be passed through to US agricultural exporters.
[NMPF](https://www.nmpf.org/u-s-dairy-statement-on-u-s-china-board-of-trade/), [Oklahoma Farm Report](https://www.oklahomafarmreport.com/2026/09/28/u-s-dairy-statement-on-u-s-china-board-of-trade/)
