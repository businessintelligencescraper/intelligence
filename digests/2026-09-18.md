### Dairy Industry

**CoBank Analysis: Ten States Now Produce 74% of US Milk as Production Consolidates** (Low · Monitor Only) — A Sept. 17, 2026 CoBank analysis by lead dairy economist Corey Geiger finds that 10 states — California, Wisconsin, Idaho, Texas, New York, Michigan, Minnesota, Pennsylvania, Iowa and New Mexico — now produce 74% of US milk (84% among the top 14), with growth concentrated in states with available water and new processing investment. Since January 2023, Texas, Idaho, Kansas, South Dakota and Michigan have added dairy cows while Wisconsin's herd has shrunk by about 44,000 head. *Why it matters:* Continued geographic consolidation of US milk supply toward the Great Plains, Southwest and parts of the Upper Midwest has direct implications for a national dairy manufacturer's sourcing, plant-siting, water-risk and logistics planning as new processing capacity follows the milk. **New Screwworm Case Confirmed in New Mexico Horse Days Before Santa Teresa Port Reopens to Mexican Cattle** (Medium · Monitor Only) — New Mexico and USDA officials confirmed Sept. 17, 2026 that a horse in Grant County tested positive for New World screwworm — the state's second case since the pest re-entered the US in June — just after Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins announced at a Santa Teresa news conference that the Santa Teresa Port of Entry will reopen to Mexican cattle imports on Sept. 24. Officials called the new case unrelated to the reopening plan and said it is unlikely to delay it; Santa Teresa has handled roughly 43% of Mexican cattle imports over the past five years. *Why it matters:* Santa Teresa would be the second and largest port reopened in the phased return of Mexican cattle after last year's screwworm-driven border closure, and a national dairy manufacturer's supply chain depends on that trade for dairy-replacement heifers; a fresh US-side detection so close to a reopening date — even one officials say won't delay the port — keeps alive the risk that further cases could pause or reverse the reopening, as a similar Sonora detection nearly did with the Douglas, AZ port in August. ### Sustainability

**California Adopts Statewide Order to Curb Dairy Manure Nitrate Pollution in Groundwater** (Medium · Monitor Only) — The California State Water Resources Control Board voted unanimously on Sept. 15, 2026 to adopt a new statewide order requiring Central Valley dairies to meet a 10 mg/L nitrate drinking-water standard, tighten manure storage and land-application practices, expand manure export to other cropland, and supply alternative drinking water to households with contaminated wells. The order sends the region's 2013 dairy waste discharge requirements back to the Central Valley Regional Water Board for revision, with final rules and phased compliance schedules due within five years. *Why it matters:* California is the top US milk-producing state, and regulators estimate about 94% of dairy-related nitrogen loading in the Central Valley comes from manure spread on cropland, so this reshapes farm-level environmental compliance costs and land-application economics across a sourcing region a national dairy manufacturer depends on. [California State Water Resources Control Board](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/water_issues/programs/enforcement/dairy_general_order.shtml), [Inside Climate News](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/18092026/california-dairy-manure-groundwater-regulation/)
