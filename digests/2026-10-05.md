**White House Reportedly Slowing MAHA's Ultra-Processed-Food Definition and School-Meal Rule Changes** — The Wall Street Journal reported, in coverage relayed Oct. 5 by The Food Institute, that the White House has slowed several Make America Healthy Again initiatives led by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., including the planned federal definition of "ultra-processed food" and a revamp of USDA school-meal guidelines, over concerns about exposure in ongoing food-related litigation and effects on international trade. A White House-blocked HHS letter supporting restrictions on certain refined carbohydrates was also cited. *Why it matters:* A federal UPF definition is the building block regulators would need before any front-of-pack warning label or category-wide marketing restriction could reach packaged dairy products like flavored milk, yogurt and cheese snacks; this reporting suggests that process is running into internal administration resistance tied to litigation and trade risk, which could push the timeline on the already-tracked OIRA review further out. It is also a signal that the administration's own agencies are not unified on scope, which is useful context for any public-affairs engagement on the definition. *Recommended action:* Brief government-affairs leadership on the reported internal White House friction; no public response needed, but track for any confirmation or denial and watch whether it shifts the OIRA review timeline on the UPF definition. **IDFA warns EU corporate due-diligence rules are raising costs for U.S. dairy exporters** (Low · Monitor Only) — The International Dairy Foods Association told U.S. trade officials that the EU's Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive and related supply-chain rules are imposing significant compliance costs on U.S. dairy processors and farmers, including some that don't sell directly into Europe. IDFA said members report initial social audits costing thousands of dollars per facility, with a full audit cycle plus follow-up approaching $27,000 per site and over 100 staff hours. *Why it matters:* Even companies without direct EU sales can be pulled into compliance costs through supply-chain due-diligence requirements from EU-facing customers or co-packers, making this a cost and resourcing issue for the broader U.S. dairy sector, not just exporters. IDFA is pressing the administration to seek a U.S. exemption from the rule's extraterritorial reach, which is the lever worth watching. [Dairy Foods](https://www.dairyfoods.com/articles/99437-idfa-eu-due-diligence-rules-are-increasing-costs-for-us-dairy), [Morning Ag Clips](https://www.morningagclips.com/idfa-warns-eu-due-diligence-rules-are-increasing-costs-for-us-dairy/)

### Nutrition

**Virginia Tech Study Finds Ultra-Processed Meals Trigger Different Metabolic and Brain-Reward Responses Than Matched Whole-Food Meals** (Medium · Potential Media Interest) — Researchers at Virginia Tech's Fralin Biomedical Research Institute published a controlled-feeding and brain-imaging study in Nature Metabolism on Oct. 5, finding that ultra-processed and minimally processed meals matched within 1% for calories, carbohydrates, fat, protein, fiber, water and salt still produced measurably different effects: the ultra-processed meal prompted more insulin release and different activation of the brain's dopaminergic reward circuitry in the same 57 adult participants. *Why it matters:* This is a rigorously controlled study, not an observational one, designed specifically to show that processing itself changes metabolic and brain-reward responses independent of nutrient content — it strengthens the scientific case behind the federal ultra-processed-food definition already on the watch list. It does not implicate dairy specifically, but could feed a UPF narrative that eventually reaches processed dairy formats like flavored milk, cheese snacks or additive-containing yogurt. [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-calories-responses-food-metabolism-brain.html), [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/ultraprocessed-food-changes-metabolic-response-study-12523675)

**USDA's Dairy Forward Pricing Program Lapses Again as Farm Bill Remains Stalled** (Low · Monitor Only) — USDA's authority for milk handlers to enter new Dairy Forward Pricing Program contracts expired Sept. 30, 2026 because Congress has not enacted a new farm bill or other legislation extending it; USDA's Agricultural Marketing Service cannot authorize new forward-pricing contracts starting Oct. 1 until authority is restored. Previously established contracts that run through Sept. 30, 2029 are unaffected. *Why it matters:* This is a second, distinct consequence of the stalled Farm Bill beyond the Sept. 30 lapse already tracked on the watch list: it removes a risk-management tool for dairy handlers and cooperatives until Congress acts, though it does not affect any contracts already in place.
