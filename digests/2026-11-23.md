### Legislative

**New York Governor Vetoes First-in-Nation Food-Chemical GRAS Disclosure Bill** (Low · Potential Media Interest) — Gov. Kathy Hochul vetoed the Food Safety and Chemical Disclosure Act (S1239/A1556) on Nov. 20, 2026, which would have required companies to publicly disclose safety data behind self-affirmed "Generally Recognized as Safe" (GRAS) determinations and banned three additives — FD&C Red 3, potassium bromate, and propylparaben — statewide. Her office cited cost impacts on small food businesses and bakers and noted that FDA has already banned Red 3 and has its own proposed rules addressing GRAS reform. Bill sponsor Assemblymember Anna Kelles said she will reintroduce the measure.
*Why it matters:* This would have been the first state law requiring disclosure of self-affirmed GRAS safety data and was widely seen as a template other states were watching; its failure removes, for now, a potential compliance burden on food and dairy manufacturers selling in New York. Advocacy groups (CSPI, Consumer Reports) are pushing back hard on the veto and the sponsor plans to reintroduce next session, so the underlying issue remains live rather than closed.
*Recommended action:* Monitor advocacy reaction and reintroduction plans; no immediate action needed since the veto maintains the regulatory status quo.
