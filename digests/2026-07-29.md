**Coca-Cola's fairlife Dairy Brand Resumes Production After Ransomware Attack Forced Plant Shutdowns** — Coca-Cola disclosed July 16 that its fairlife, LLC dairy subsidiary detected unauthorized third-party access to its systems, including production systems, in a ransomware event; a group calling itself Anubis claimed responsibility, and production was temporarily suspended at fairlife's four US facilities. On July 27, Coca-Cola announced fairlife had resumed the majority of production, said product quality and safety were not affected, and that retail availability was largely unimpacted due to existing inventory; the company said it does not expect a material financial impact.
*Why it matters:* A ransomware attack that halted production at a national dairy processor's plants for over a week is a live case study in operational-technology cyber risk for dairy manufacturing — a direct precedent for the company's own IT/OT security posture, incident response, and business-continuity/inventory planning; it also shows a major dairy brand can face retail-availability and reputation questions from a cyber incident even absent any food-safety issue, a natural media and analyst question ("could this happen at your plants?").
*Recommended action:* Share as an external case study with IT/OT security and business-continuity leadership; monitor for any disclosed detail on attack vector or lessons applicable to plant control systems. **Appeals Court Revives Class Action Over Chobani's 'Zero Sugar' Yogurt Claims, Says Allulose Counts as Sugar** — The 7th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals in Chicago ruled July 27 that a proposed class action against Chobani over its Chobani Zero Sugar yogurt can proceed, rejecting Chobani's argument that allulose — a low-calorie sweetener the yogurt contains at 4 grams per serving — doesn't count toward FDA's threshold for "sugar free"/"zero sugar" label claims. The panel credited an FDA amicus brief stating that "total sugars" on Nutrition Facts panels includes all monosaccharides, including allulose, and sent the case back to a Chicago federal judge.
*Why it matters:* This is a reputable-court interpretation of what counts as "sugar" for FDA label-claim purposes; any dairy product using allulose or a comparable novel sweetener while making "sugar free," "zero sugar," or "no added sugar" claims is now on notice that those claims may not survive scrutiny, making it worth a quick legal/regulatory-affairs check of the company's own labeling wherever such sweeteners or claims are used.
*Recommended action:* Have legal/regulatory affairs review any of the company's "sugar free"/"zero sugar" claims for allulose or comparable sweeteners against this ruling before it becomes a media or plaintiff's-bar talking point.
