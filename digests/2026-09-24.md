## New developments

### Dairy Industry

**CoBank: Higher-Component Dairy Herds Earn Up to $474 More Per Cow** (Low · Monitor Only) — A Sept. 24, 2026 CoBank Knowledge Exchange report finds dairy herds in the top decile for milk components (butterfat and protein) earn $101-$352 more per cow annually at average U.S. production levels, rising to $136-$474 per cow at higher production levels, as more than 80% of farmgate milk now moves into manufactured dairy products (cheese, yogurt, butter, cottage cheese) rather than fluid milk. The analysis covered seven regional Federal Milk Marketing Orders that use Multiple Component Pricing. *Why it matters:* As the category's mix keeps shifting toward manufactured dairy products, component levels rather than fluid volume increasingly drive both farmer milk-check economics and processors' ability to secure the butterfat and protein their plants need -- relevant background for sourcing and producer-relations strategy, though it carries no regulatory or reputational dimension. [GlobeNewswire (CoBank)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/24/3368629/0/en/higher-component-dairy-herds-capture-bigger-milk-checks-as-consumer-demand-shifts-toward-manufactured-dairy-products.html), [Red River Farm Network](https://www.rrfn.com/2026/09/24/consumer-dairy-demand-shifts/)

**SunOpta (Now Part of Refresco) Opens $35M Plant-Based Milk Production Line in Texas** (Low · Monitor Only) — SunOpta, now part of beverage co-packer Refresco, opened a fourth production line at its Midlothian, Texas facility, a $35 million investment expected to grow the plant's plant-based milk and creamer output capacity by about 10%. *Why it matters:* Continued capacity investment by a major plant-based co-packer is a reminder that plant-based alternatives remain a well-funded competitor for shelf space and consumer share against dairy milk and creamers, even as category growth has slowed nationally. [Dairy Business (Middle East & Africa)](https://dairybusinessmea.com/2026/09/23/sunopta-opens-its-us35m-expanded-production-line/), [Vegan-News.net](https://www.vegan-news.net/now-part-of-refresco-sunopta-opens-35m-production-line-for-plant-based-dairy)

### Food Industry

**Non-GMO Project's 'Non-UPF Verified' Label Surpasses 300 Certified Products** (Low · Monitor Only) — The Non-GMO Project announced Sept. 18, 2026 that its nine-month-old "Non-UPF Verified" third-party certification has grown to more than 300 products across 23 brands (including Simple Mills, Chomps and Blue Diamond) spanning 15 food and beverage categories, with 41 more brands seeking verification; the program is also launching a shopper-facing barcode-scanning app. *Why it matters:* A private, non-governmental non-UPF certification is scaling in parallel with state efforts like California's AB 2244 seal, giving manufacturers — including makers of flavored dairy, cultured and processed cheese products — a voluntary label option ahead of any binding state or federal UPF standard; worth tracking as a possible template or complication for the FDA's own pending UPF definition. [PR Newswire (Non-GMO Project)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/non-upf-verified-surpasses-300-products-as-demand-for-food-processing-transparency-grows-302883048.html), [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/non-gmo-upf-label-expands-chomps-blue-diamond/830894/)

**Packaged-Food Makers Flag Ultra-Processed-Food Scrutiny as a Financial Risk in SEC Filings** (Low · Monitor Only) — Campbell's, PepsiCo, Conagra, Kraft Heinz and J.M. Smucker's latest SEC 10-K filings — Campbell's filed Sept. 24, 2026 — disclose that growing regulatory and consumer scrutiny of "ultra-processed foods," including MAHA Commission proposals and state legislative activity, could lead to new definitions, labeling or marketing requirements or reformulation mandates that raise costs or reduce demand; several companies also disclose pending UPF-related litigation risk. *Why it matters:* These are among the first major packaged-food companies to name UPF scrutiny as a standalone, material investor risk factor, a sign the issue has moved from a nutrition-advocacy debate into something food and dairy manufacturers are now expected to formally account for in their own risk disclosures. [FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/09/24/big-food-warns-of-risks-from-ultra-processed-foods/), [Campbell's 10-K (SEC EDGAR)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000016732/000001673226000026/cpb-20260802.htm)
