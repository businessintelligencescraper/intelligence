### Dairy Industry

**CoBank: Higher-Component Dairy Herds Earn Up to $474 More Per Cow** (Low · Monitor Only) — A Sept. 24, 2026 CoBank Knowledge Exchange report finds dairy herds in the top decile for milk components (butterfat and protein) earn $101-$352 more per cow annually at average U.S. production levels, rising to $136-$474 per cow at higher production levels, as more than 80% of farmgate milk now moves into manufactured dairy products (cheese, yogurt, butter, cottage cheese) rather than fluid milk. The analysis covered seven regional Federal Milk Marketing Orders that use Multiple Component Pricing. *Why it matters:* As the category's mix keeps shifting toward manufactured dairy products, component levels rather than fluid volume increasingly drive both farmer milk-check economics and processors' ability to secure the butterfat and protein their plants need -- relevant background for sourcing and producer-relations strategy, though it carries no regulatory or reputational dimension. ### Food Industry

**Non-GMO Project's 'Non-UPF Verified' Label Surpasses 300 Certified Products** (Low · Monitor Only) — The Non-GMO Project announced Sept. 18, 2026 that its nine-month-old "Non-UPF Verified" third-party certification has grown to more than 300 products across 23 brands (including Simple Mills, Chomps and Blue Diamond) spanning 15 food and beverage categories, with 41 more brands seeking verification; the program is also launching a shopper-facing barcode-scanning app. *Why it matters:* A private, non-governmental non-UPF certification is scaling in parallel with state efforts like California's AB 2244 seal, giving manufacturers — including makers of flavored dairy, cultured and processed cheese products — a voluntary label option ahead of any binding state or federal UPF standard; worth tracking as a possible template or complication for the FDA's own pending UPF definition.
