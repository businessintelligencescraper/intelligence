## New developments

### Dairy Industry
**Family Farmstead Dairy Recalls Raw Cheddar Again, This Time for Listeria** (Medium risk · Potential Media Interest) — Family Farmstead Dairy of Worcester, New York voluntarily recalled its Raw Mild and Raw Sharp Cheddar Cheese (8-oz packages, lot codes dated 10/16/27 or earlier) on Nov. 10 after testing found possible Listeria monocytogenes contamination. The cheese was sold at retail in 12 Northeast and Mid-Atlantic states; no illnesses had been reported as of the announcement. It is the company's second cheddar recall in under a month, following an Oct. 15 recall of a single lot over E. coli contamination. *Why it matters:* A second recall from the same small raw-milk cheesemaker in under a month, this time for Listeria and across a wider footprint, adds another data point to the raw-milk-cheese safety pattern that FDA's pending warning-label petition and advocacy groups are already citing; it keeps media and regulatory attention on raw-milk dairy safety generally, though it does not implicate pasteurized dairy products or major processors. [Food Poisoning Bulletin](https://foodpoisoningbulletin.com/2026/family-farmstead-raw-cheddar-cheese-recalled-for-listeria/), [Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/life/recalls/cheddar-cheese-recalled-in-12-states-over-possible-listeria-contamination)

**Oregon Confirms State's First H5N1 Case in Dairy Cattle** (Medium risk · Potential Media Interest) — USDA's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service confirmed H5N1 avian influenza in a commercial dairy herd in Morrow County, Oregon on Nov. 10, 2026, the state's first detection in dairy cattle since the national outbreak began in March 2024. The herd was flagged through routine milk surveillance testing after previously testing negative; Oregon's state veterinarian called the detection significant but not unexpected given the virus's spread nationally. *Why it matters:* Oregon joins a growing list of dairy states reporting H5N1 in cattle, arriving just weeks after the first US human bird-flu death of 2026 was linked to dairy-cattle exposure in Washington, which has kept "dairy cattle" paired with bird-flu headlines in regional and trade media. CDC and state officials say general public risk remains low and pasteurized milk remains safe, but the category should expect continued local media interest and possible worker-safety questions in the Pacific Northwest. [OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/11/12/oregon-first-case-bird-flu-dairy-cattle/), [Capital Press](https://capitalpress.com/2026/11/10/bird-flu-hits-oregon-dairy-herd-as-virus-strikes-northwest/), [KOIN](https://www.koin.com/news/oregon/bird-flu-detected-in-oregon-dairy-cattle-for-first-time-officials-say/)
