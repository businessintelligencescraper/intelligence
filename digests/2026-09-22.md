### Dairy Industry

**IDFA and Ice Cream Makers Mark Progress Toward 2027 Artificial-Color Elimination Pledge** (Low · Monitor Only) — The International Dairy Foods Association joined USDA Secretary Brooke Rollins and HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. at USDA headquarters on Sept. 22 to highlight ice cream makers' progress toward a voluntary pledge, first announced in July 2025, to eliminate seven certified artificial colors from ice cream and frozen dairy desserts made with real milk by Dec. 31, 2027. IDFA says roughly 40 companies representing more than 90% of U.S. ice cream volume have signed on. *Why it matters:* This is the third industry-wide voluntary commitment IDFA has coordinated for the dairy sector (after added-sugar reduction and school-dairy color removal), keeping the category aligned with the administration's food-additive agenda ahead of any binding FDA color rule. Companies not already part of the pledge, or lagging on reformulation timelines, face rising comparison pressure as peers publicize progress. ### Sustainability

**UK Packaging-Fee Data Shows Flexible Plastic Packaging Facing Sharply Higher Recycling Costs** (Low · Monitor Only) — New recyclability assessment data published under the UK's extended producer responsibility (pEPR) packaging scheme found that nearly two-thirds of flexible plastic packaging assessed in 2025 received the lowest recyclability rating, which will carry materially higher waste-disposal fees than better-rated packaging once fee modulation begins. Industry sources cited in the coverage estimated it could cost roughly $40 million to convert a single mid-size packaging line to a higher-rated format. *Why it matters:* Flexible pouches and films are common dairy ingredient and consumer packaging formats; while this fee structure is UK-specific, it is a concrete, dollars-and-cents example of how recyclability-based fee modulation can raise packaging costs once it takes effect — the same design being debated in US state EPR programs (California SB 54, Oregon, Colorado) that already sit on the watch list. [DairyReporter](https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2026/09/18/recyclability-rules-could-make-flexible-plastics-more-expensive-for-producers/)
