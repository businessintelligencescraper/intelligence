### Legislative

**New York's GRAS Food-Chemical Disclosure Bill Formally Reaches Governor Hochul's Desk, Starting Veto Clock** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — New York's Legislature formally delivered the Food Safety and Chemical Disclosure Act (S.1239/A.1556) to Gov. Kathy Hochul on Nov. 13, 2026, starting her constitutional 10-day (excluding Sundays) window to sign or veto. If she takes no action by Nov. 25, 2026, the bill becomes law without her signature. The bill bans three additives (FD&C Red No. 3, potassium bromate, and propylparaben) and would be the first state law requiring public disclosure of the safety basis for ingredients cleared only through the self-affirmed GRAS pathway.
*Why it matters:* This is the concrete deadline the GRAS-disclosure fight has been building toward all year: IDFA has urged a veto while CSPI and legislative sponsors press for a signature, and the outcome would set a template other states (and a slower-moving FDA GRAS-reform effort) could follow for self-affirmed-GRAS ingredients used across packaged dairy products.
*Recommended action:* Brief leadership ahead of the Nov. 25 deadline and have a short public position ready for either a signature or a veto. [PackagingLaw.com](https://www.packaginglaw.com/news/new-york-gras-bill-formally-presented-governor)

### Dairy Industry

**Oklahoma Confirms First H5N1 Dairy-Herd Case Since 2024** (Medium · Potential Media Interest) — USDA APHIS confirmed on Nov. 16, 2026 that highly pathogenic avian influenza (H5N1) was detected in an Oklahoma dairy herd of roughly 3,000-4,000 cows, the state's first dairy-cattle case since 2024. The Oklahoma Department of Agriculture, Food and Forestry said the sample was flagged by the state's animal disease diagnostic lab and has not disclosed the herd's county or location.
*Why it matters:* Oklahoma is at least the third state (after Indiana on Nov. 2 and Oregon on Nov. 10) to report its first H5N1 dairy-herd detection within two weeks, extending a fall resurgence that has already prompted state animal-health officials to press USDA for an emergency vaccine pilot. Officials emphasize pasteurization keeps the commercial milk supply safe, but continued spread to new states keeps biosecurity and consumer-confidence questions live for the category.
*Recommended action:* Monitor coverage and state/USDA biosecurity guidance; no company-specific action needed absent a milk-supply-safety challenge. [High Plains Journal](https://hpj.com/2026/11/16/hpai-confirmed-in-oklahoma-dairy-herd/), [KOSU](https://www.kosu.org/local-news/2026-11-17/bird-flu-found-in-oklahoma-dairy-herd)
