**USDA Sets 2027 Dairy Import License Fee at $325, Updates Safeguard Trigger Levels** (Low · Monitor Only) — USDA's Foreign Agricultural Service published a Federal Register notice Sept. 2 setting the 2027 tariff-rate-quota-year dairy import license fee at $325 per license, down from $350 in 2026, based on an estimated $631,516 total administrative cost spread across roughly 1,950 expected licenses. A companion notice updated the quantity-based safeguard trigger levels and license-transfer amounts that apply to dairy products under the 2027 TRQ year. *Why it matters:* Routine annual administrative notice for companies that hold USDA dairy import licenses under the tariff-rate-quota system; no policy change, but the updated safeguard trigger levels are a detail import-dependent processors should log for 2027 planning. ### Dairy Industry

**Chobani to Invest $1.2 Billion in New Pennsylvania Milk-Processing Hub** (Low · Potential Media Interest) — Chobani announced Sept. 1 it will invest approximately $1.2 billion over five years to convert a former Keurig Dr Pepper facility in Allentown, Pennsylvania into a 1.5-million-square-foot milk production and warehouse campus, creating more than 900 jobs. Gov. ### Sustainability

**House Subcommittee Advances Bipartisan Bills to Federalize Packaging 'Recyclable' and 'Recycled Content' Claims** (Medium · Monitor Only) — On Sept. 1, 2026, the House Energy and Commerce Committee's Commerce, Manufacturing and Trade Subcommittee voice-voted to advance the PACK Act (H.R. 6832) and the Recycled Materials Attribution Act (H.R. 7502) to the full committee. The bills would set federal, third-party-certified standards for "recyclable," "compostable," "reusable" and "recycled content" packaging claims and would preempt the current patchwork of state rules; RMAA would also formally recognize mass-balance accounting for recycled-content claims. *Why it matters:* A single federal packaging-claims standard would ease compliance for a national dairy manufacturer navigating inconsistent state EPR and labeling laws, but the mass-balance provision is already drawing greenwashing criticism from environmental advocates. [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/policy-now/2026/09/01/house-subcommittee-advances-pack-act/), [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/09/04/cpgs-eye-relief-from-packaging-claims-patchwork/)

**EPA and Army Corps Reopen Public Comment on WOTUS Water Definition Ahead of Final Rule** (Low · Monitor Only) — On Sept. 4, 2026, EPA and the US Army Corps of Engineers issued a Supplemental Notice of Proposed Rulemaking seeking additional public input on how to define "waters of the United States" (WOTUS) under the Clean Water Act, including the definitions of "relatively permanent" waters and a "continuous surface water connection." The agencies say they are advancing toward a "durable final rule" after receiving more than 22,000 comments on their original November 2025 proposal; the new comment window opens for 30 days once the supplemental notice is published in the Federal Register, expected within about a week. *Why it matters:* WOTUS jurisdiction determines which farm ditches, wetlands and on-site waters require Clean Water Act permits before discharge or fill activity, directly affecting dairy farms and processing facilities that manage wastewater or stormwater; this fresh comment window is a chance to weigh in before the definition is finalized.
