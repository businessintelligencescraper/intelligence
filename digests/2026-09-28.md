## ⚠ Priority items

**California Governor Signs AB 2244, Creating First-in-Nation "Non-Ultraprocessed Certified" Food Label** — Gov. Gavin Newsom signed AB 2244 on Sept. 28, 2026, establishing a voluntary "Non-Ultraprocessed Certified" seal that the California Department of Public Health will oversee, with third-party certifiers accredited by June 1, 2029 and recertification required every three years. Products containing synthetic dyes, flavor enhancers, non-sugar sweeteners or certain processing aids will not qualify. The bill passed the Legislature unanimously (32-0 Senate, 72-0 Assembly) on Sept. 1; the signature resolves weeks of uncertainty after an earlier report of Newsom's signature in mid-September proved premature.
*Why it matters:* This is the first state-run certification seal built around a legal ultra-processed-food definition, and it directly touches flavored dairy and processed cheese formulations that rely on flavor enhancers, non-sugar sweeteners or processing aids. It is likely to be studied as a template by other states, and gives retailers and advocacy groups a concrete on-pack marker to promote — though participation is voluntary and certifier accreditation is not required until mid-2029, so there is no near-term compliance deadline.
*Recommended action:* Brief product development, regulatory affairs and marketing on the certification criteria and formulation implications; track CDPH's implementing rulemaking and any similar bills introduced in other states; no immediate compliance action needed given the voluntary program and 2029 accreditation timeline. ### Nutrition

**Stanford Study: California's School Ultra-Processed Food Phase-Out Faces Budget and Staffing Hurdles** (Low · Monitor Only) — A Stanford Medicine study published in Public Health Nutrition surveyed 20 food-service directors in California's San Joaquin Valley on implementing AB 1264, the state's 2025 law requiring public schools to phase out certain ultra-processed foods between July 2029 and July 2035. Directors cited tight per-meal reimbursement (about $4.60 under universal meals), part-time "heat-and-serve" staffing, vendor shortages for compliant ingredients, and costly kitchen upgrades needed for scratch cooking as the main barriers, and researchers said additional funding will likely be needed to meet the deadline.
*Why it matters:* This is the first close look at real-world feasibility for the nation's first statutory school ultra-processed-food phase-out, and its funding/timeline findings could inform amendments to AB 1264 or shape how other states calibrate similar bills. [U.S. News & World Report](https://www.usnews.com/news/health-news/articles/2026-09-28/eliminating-ultra-processed-food-from-school-cafeterias-will-be-costly-complicated-study-warns), [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-09-ultra-food-school-menus-tricky.html)
