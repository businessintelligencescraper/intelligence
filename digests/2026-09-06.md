## ⚠ Priority items

**ByHeart and IDFA Funded the FDA-Adjacent Report Reviewing Their Own Infant-Formula Botulism Outbreaks** — The Reagan-Udall Foundation for the FDA published "Applying the Science of Infant Formula Safety," a 44-page report stemming from a July 13, 2026 roundtable on Clostridium botulinum in powdered infant formula that was convened in collaboration with, and paid for by, ByHeart and the International Dairy Foods Association (IDFA) — the company and dairy trade association tied to the 2025-2026 botulism outbreaks that hospitalized roughly 50 infants. The report issues 22 non-binding recommendations on testing and prevention; none of its 24 listed contributors is a parent of an affected infant.
*Why it matters:* IDFA speaks for the US dairy manufacturing industry broadly, so press criticism that the trade association and an implicated company funded a review of their own outbreak — with no mandatory rule proposed and no affected families involved — is a reputational exposure that can extend to dairy processors generally, particularly around industry self-funded science and testing-standard credibility (the report found the legacy SRC test can miss contamination that PCR testing catches). Any manufacturer relying on IDFA's advocacy or sharing organic milk-powder suppliers with the earlier outbreaks should expect continued questions about industry-funded safety reviews.
*Recommended action:* Brief communications/government-affairs leadership on the funding-transparency criticism before reporters ask whether the company backs IDFA's role; confirm no shared exposure via the Organic West Milk/Dairy Farmers of America supply chain already tracked from the Nara Organics item.

**USDA Unveils Plan to Modernize Agricultural Data Collection** (Low · Monitor Only) — USDA Secretary Brooke Rollins announced a "Data Modernization Plan" at the Farm Progress Show on Sept. 1, aiming to reduce producer reporting burden, expand use of administrative data and mobile survey access, and modernize data infrastructure and geospatial tools.
*Why it matters:* USDA collects extensive mandatory and voluntary data from dairy processors; how the plan is implemented could change reporting formats, frequency, or burden, though no specific new dairy requirement was announced. ### Dairy Industry

**IDFA-Backed SNAP Milk Incentive Program "Add Milk!" Expands to Oklahoma** (Low · Potential Media Interest) — The Healthy Fluid Milk Incentive project "Add Milk!," championed by IDFA and funded via a $2.99M USDA grant to Auburn University's Hunger Solutions Institute, launched at three Tulsa-area stores in late August, giving SNAP participants a dollar-for-dollar match on qualifying low-fat, non-fat and lactose-free milk; the program now reaches more than 1,200 stores across 32 states.
*Why it matters:* A positive, verifiable industry-backed nutrition-access story that gives dairy manufacturers a concrete talking point on milk affordability and SNAP access. [Cheese Reporter](https://cheesereporter.com/news/food-safety-health/2026/09/04/snap-milk-incentive-program-expands-to-oklahoma/), [Dairy Business News](https://dairybusiness.com/add-milk-snap-dairy-incentive-program-expands-to-oklahoma/)
