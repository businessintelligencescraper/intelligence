**FDA/USDA Criticized for Delayed, Informal Public Naming of Companies Tied to Foodborne Outbreaks** (Medium · Potential Media Interest) — A Food Safety News "Publisher's Platform" column (Oct. 25) reports that USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service confirmed to USA Today on Oct. 23 that Trader Joe's Tarragon Chicken Salad Wraps are linked to an E. coli O157:H7 outbreak that has sickened at least 50 people — but did so only in an email statement, not a press release or updated outbreak page, roughly a month after FDA and the retailer were first notified in September. The disclosure follows a separate Oct. 16 citizen petition filed with FDA by food-safety attorney Bill Marler, backed by STOP Foodborne Illness, asking the agency to name firms implicated in outbreaks even when no recall occurs. *Why it matters:* The episode and the pending petition both push toward faster, more public naming of companies tied to outbreaks before any formal recall — a policy shift that would raise reputational exposure for any food or dairy manufacturer swept into a future outbreak investigation, including ones resembling the recent Sierra Nevada Graziers raw-milk cheese cases.
