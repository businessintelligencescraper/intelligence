## ⚠ Priority items

**FDA's Proposed Rule Ending Self-Affirmed GRAS Notification Clears White House Review** (High · Executive Awareness Required) — FDA's proposed rule to require mandatory notification for most GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) food-substance determinations completed OMB/OIRA review, per a notice posted July 31, 2026. The rule (RIN 0910-AJ02) would end reliance on purely self-affirmed GRAS status for most substances and require FDA to maintain a public GRAS-notice inventory; the 2026 Unified Regulatory Agenda lists Federal Register publication as anticipated for December 2026.
*Why it matters:* This would be the largest change to the GRAS pathway in decades, affecting nearly every food and dairy ingredient supplier that relies on self-affirmed GRAS determinations for enzymes, cultures, flavors, and functional ingredients. It runs on a parallel federal track to the New York state GRAS-disclosure bill IDFA is already fighting, so both fronts should be briefed together once the proposed rule text and comment period are public.
*Recommended action:* Flag to ingredient/regulatory-affairs teams now; prepare to brief leadership when the proposed rule and comment period publish, expected around December 2026. [Food Safety Magazine](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/11703-regulatory-updates-gras-proposed-rule-clears-omb-review-fda-sends-upf-whitepaper-to-oira), [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/2026-regulatory-agenda-released-updates-information-regarding-gras-rulemaking)

**Senate Agriculture Committee Schedules Aug. 6 Farm Bill Markup With New Mandatory Dairy Cost-Reporting Provision** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — Senate Agriculture Committee Chairman John Boozman (R-AR) released updated text of the "Agricultural Act of 2026" (Farm Bill 2.0) and scheduled a full committee markup for Thursday, Aug. 6. The dairy title would require dairy product manufacturers to report production cost and yield data to USDA every two years to update the "make allowance" factors used in Federal Milk Marketing Order pricing formulas, and reauthorizes the Dairy Indemnity Payment Program and Dairy Promotion and Research Board through 2031.
*Why it matters:* A mandatory manufacturer cost/yield reporting requirement would be a new direct compliance obligation tied to how FMMO minimum prices are set — a mechanism the industry has debated for years; the markup is the first concrete legislative test of whether it advances.
*Recommended action:* Monitor markup outcome Thursday; brief leadership if amendments expand the reporting scope. [Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition & Forestry](https://www.agriculture.senate.gov/newsroom/rep/press/release/chairman-boozman-releases-updated-farm-bill-20-text-announces-business-meeting), [Southeast AgNET](https://southeastagnet.com/2026/08/04/senate-farm-bill-reaches-key-milestone/), [Feedstuffs](https://www.feedstuffs.com/policy/senate-to-mark-up-farm-bill-this-thursday)

**FDA Sends Ultra-Processed Food Definition Whitepaper to White House Review** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — FDA and HHS on August 3 sent a whitepaper, "Proposed Definition of Ultra-Processed Food," to OIRA. The submission is filed as a notice rather than a proposed rule, carries no legal deadline, and is not designated economically significant; HHS Secretary Kennedy has said the definition could become the basis for a red-yellow-green front-of-pack labeling system.
*Why it matters:* A federal UPF definition would be the foundational building block for any future front-of-pack labeling mandate or program restriction; it advances in parallel with two live Senate warning-label bills, so dairy categories spanning flavored milk, ice cream, and processed cheese should expect continued scrutiny even though no rule or deadline exists yet.
*Recommended action:* Track OIRA review progress; no immediate action required. [Food Safety Magazine](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/11703-regulatory-updates-gras-proposed-rule-clears-omb-review-fda-sends-upf-whitepaper-to-oira)

**Coca-Cola Confirms Data Theft in fairlife Ransomware Attack** (Medium · Executive Awareness Required) — Coca-Cola confirmed that the ransomware attack on its fairlife dairy subsidiary, first disclosed July 16, involved theft of company data, after the Anubis ransomware group claimed on July 20 to have exfiltrated roughly 1 terabyte of confidential data. Coca-Cola has not confirmed the volume or nature of the stolen data; the company says product quality and safety were not affected.
*Why it matters:* This escalates the fairlife incident from an operational-technology/production-continuity case study into a data-breach and disclosure matter that can trigger breach-notification obligations and litigation well after production resumes; early data-breach litigation inquiries have already surfaced.
*Recommended action:* Share with IT/security and legal as an update to the fairlife case study; confirm the company's own breach-notification protocol addresses both OT disruption and data-theft scenarios. [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/28/coca-cola-fairlife-dairy-subsidiary-ransomware-attack/), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/coca-cola-confirms-data-theft-in-fairlife-ransomware-attack/)

*Why it matters:* A routine but useful reminder that FDA continues active FSVP enforcement against companies importing dairy ingredients; worth flagging to supply-chain/import-compliance teams to confirm FSVP documentation is current for any imported milk powder, whey, casein, or other dairy inputs, particularly as the looming Aug. 19 Section 338 tariff on Canadian dairy inputs may push some processors toward new import suppliers.
