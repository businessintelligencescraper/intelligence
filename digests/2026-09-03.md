## ⚠ Priority items

**California Legislature Passes First-of-Its-Kind 'Non-Ultraprocessed Certified' Food Label Bill** — California's Assembly Bill 2244 cleared the Legislature on Aug. 31 with unanimous votes (32-0 in the Senate, 72-0 in the Assembly) and was sent to Gov. Gavin Newsom on Sept. 1. The bill creates a voluntary state certification seal, "Non-Ultraprocessed Certified," for products meeting standards that exclude synthetic dyes, flavor enhancers, non-sugar sweeteners and certain processing aids; Newsom has until Sept. 30, 2026 to sign or veto it and is reportedly expected to sign.
*Why it matters:* This would be the first state-level certification program built around a UPF definition, giving California a template other states may copy; because the qualifying criteria touch additives, flavor enhancers, non-sugar sweeteners and processing aids common in flavored milk, cultured dairy and processed cheese, it creates both a potential marketing opportunity for compliant products and a competitive/labeling distinction for products that would not qualify.
*Recommended action:* Track Governor Newsom's decision ahead of the Sept. 30 deadline; begin assessing which products could plausibly qualify for the seal once certification standards are finalized.

**CDC/FDA Close Second Infant-Formula Botulism Outbreak, Trace It to Same Dairy Supplier as Prior Outbreak** — CDC and FDA formally closed the investigation into a multistate infant botulism outbreak linked to Nara Organics powdered infant formula (four infants hospitalized in California, Pennsylvania and Washington, onset April-May 2026, no deaths) after whole-genome sequencing and supply-chain records traced the contamination to the same two suppliers implicated in the 2025 ByHeart infant formula botulism outbreak, which hospitalized 48 infants in 19 states: Organic West Milk (raw organic milk) and Dairy Farmers of America (spray-drying). FDA also noted that Organic West Milk had earlier given the agency an incomplete customer list during the ByHeart investigation that omitted Nara Organics.
*Why it matters:* This is the second national infant-health outbreak traced to the same organic whole-milk supply chain within about a year, and FDA's finding that a supplier withheld a customer name during an active federal investigation raises the likelihood of tighter agency scrutiny of organic dairy ingredient sourcing, spray-drying practices and supplier traceability industry-wide. No product or facility of the company is implicated, but any manufacturer sourcing organic milk powder, or working with the same cooperative network, should expect closer buyer due-diligence and possible new FDA traceability expectations.
*Recommended action:* Brief supply-chain/quality leadership on the Organic West Milk-Dairy Farmers of America link; confirm no shared sourcing exposure and monitor for any FDA follow-up guidance on organic dairy ingredient traceability.
[CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/botulism/outbreaks-investigations/infant-formula-june-2026/index.html), [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-infant-botulism-powdered-infant-formula-june-2026), [Food Safety News](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2026/08/federal-officials-close-investigation-into-outbreak-of-infant-botulism-traced-to-formula/), [Food Safety Magazine](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/11771-nara-organics-infant-formula-botulism-outbreak-ends-wgs-supply-chain-evidence-show-link-to-byheart)
