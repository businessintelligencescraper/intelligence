**Senate Committee Advances Bill Requiring Warning Labels on High-Sugar, High-Fat, High-Sodium Foods** — The Senate HELP Committee voted 12-10 to advance Sen. Bernie Sanders' “Childhood Diabetes Reduction Act of 2026,” which would require front-of-pack warning labels on packaged foods and beverages high in added sugar, saturated fat, sodium, or certain synthetic additives/high-intensity sweeteners, and would ban “junk food” advertising directed at children. The bipartisan committee vote sends the bill to the full Senate; FoodNavigator-USA's July 29 analysis is the latest trade-press coverage of its progress.
*Why it matters:* A federal bill requiring saturated-fat and sugar warning labels would directly implicate whole milk, butter, full-fat cheese, and sweetened dairy products across the category, creating packaging, reformulation, and messaging exposure well beyond the current voluntary UPF debate; a bipartisan committee vote signals this has moved from advocacy talking point to live legislative text headed for a Senate floor vote.
*Recommended action:* Brief legal/regulatory affairs now on which SKUs would trigger the bill's sugar/saturated-fat/sodium warning-label thresholds while it is still pre-floor-vote; coordinate messaging posture with IDFA. [FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/07/29/upf-warning-label-bill-clears-senate-panel/)

**IDFA Urges New York Governor to Veto First-in-Nation GRAS Ingredient Disclosure Law** — IDFA sent a letter dated July 28 to New York Gov. Kathy Hochul urging her to veto S.1239F/A.1556G, the “Food Safety and Chemical Disclosure Act,” which would require manufacturers to disclose safety data for self-affirmed GRAS ingredients sold in New York and would ban Red Dye 3, potassium bromate, and propylparaben. The bill passed the Assembly 106-32 on April 21 after unanimous Senate passage March 23, and now awaits the Governor's signature or veto, due within 10 days (excluding Sundays) of formal presentment.
*Why it matters:* This would be the first state law mandating manufacturer disclosure of self-affirmed GRAS ingredient safety data — a direct compliance and transparency precedent for any dairy product sold in New York, and a likely template for similar bills in other states; IDFA's veto push shows where the trade association is spending its political capital on the industry's behalf, and the outcome will shape whether ingredient-disclosure fights spread beyond New York.
*Recommended action:* Track the Governor's decision window and align public position with IDFA; have regulatory affairs inventory which company ingredients rely on self-affirmed GRAS status in case the law is signed. [IDFA](https://www.idfa.org/news/idfa-submits-letter-in-support-of-new-yorks-veto-letter-on-gras-bill)
