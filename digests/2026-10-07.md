## New developments

### Dairy Industry

**Consumer Class Action Accuses Dairy Cooperatives' Export Program of Price-Fixing** (Medium · Monitor Only) — A proposed federal consumer class action, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia, accuses the National Milk Producers Federation and 14 major dairy marketing cooperatives of using their NEXT Program (formerly Cooperatives Working Together) export-assistance program to restrict the domestic raw milk supply and inflate U.S. dairy prices. The named cooperatives collectively represent roughly 70% of U.S. milk production; plaintiffs allege Sherman Act and state antitrust/consumer-protection violations and seek injunctive relief and damages on behalf of consumer purchasers.
*Why it matters:* This is the latest in a decade-plus string of antitrust suits targeting dairy cooperative supply-management programs, several of which have ended in multi-million-dollar settlements; given the program's broad industry participation, any manufacturer sourcing milk through NEXT-participating cooperatives should watch for discovery findings or a certified class that could affect milk supply costs. It is an early-stage filing with no ruling yet, so near-term business impact is limited.
